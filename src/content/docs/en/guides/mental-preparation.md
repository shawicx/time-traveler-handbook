# Mental Preparation and Desensitization

> Era guides (e.g. the [[xia-dynasty|Xia Dynasty]]) assume your mind will hold. **It will not,
> not automatically.** "Psychological collapse" appears on no cause-of-death list, yet it
> amplifies every other cause: degraded judgment → wrong decision → death.
> This guide is universal across eras.

## 1. Sensory Shock (the Most Underestimated Part)

### Smell

A hearth that never goes out, humans and livestock sharing one room, manure heaps inside the
living area, rot without refrigeration or sanitation, bodies that have not bathed in years —
**and within three months, you are part of that smell**.

**Desensitization**: rehearse these scenes deliberately in your imagination before departure,
and complete the cognitive reframing in advance: this is not "filth," it is background noise.
Once disgust stops consuming your attention, it stays available for actual threats.

### Parasites

Lice, fleas, and intestinal worms are near-certain fate. The strategy is **management**,
not resistance:

- Regular combing and nit-picking — **mutual delousing is a social ritual; participate,
  do not resist it.**
- Boil clothing when possible (boiling water kills lice eggs).
- Mugwort smoke in the dwelling and chinaberry-bark washes have some repellent effect.
- Accepting "coexistence with vermin" as the baseline is itself the strongest defense.

### Food monotony

Millet porridge, roasted millet, boiled millet — meat at rituals, for months or years.
Run a "one staple for one week" drill before departure: experience how monotony erodes
mood, and find your coping mechanism now.

### Darkness and silence

After sunset the world is almost pitch black (moonlight is the only public lighting); the
night holds only wind, animals, and voices. Moderns experience this as near sensory
deprivation. Drill: **spend a full night alone with no light source** (in a safe setting first).

### Pain

A world without analgesics: toothache can last months; strain is daily. Reframe in advance —
pain is a **signal**, not a catastrophe.

## 2. Isolation and Cognitive Maintenance

### Linguistic isolation

Months without a single deep conversation makes thinking feel "dull." Countermeasures —
**manufacture cognitive stimulation**:

- Keep a log by knot-tying or carved marks (which conveniently reinforces your
  "spirit-toucher" persona).
- Mental arithmetic, recitation: "teach" the modern knowledge in your head to an imaginary
  apprentice — one day the apprentice will be real.

### Sense of time

No calendar, no clock. Losing track of time is the first step to losing grip on reality.
**Carve a notch on a stick or bone at every sunrise; mark every thirtieth day.**
This matters far more than it looks.

### Persona-performance fatigue

You can never relax into "being yourself" — that alone is chronic psychological wear.
The countermeasure is internal separation: treat the "shaman-healer" as a **work role**
while keeping an inner observer perspective; reserve a daily slot of solitude
(fuel-gathering, water-fetching) as "self time."

### Depression self-check and self-rescue

Warning signs: persistent difficulty rising, loss of interest in everything, self-destructive
urges. Countermeasures:

- One **small daily goal** (learn a word, fix a tool).
- Fixed routine — structure is the anchor against disintegration.
- Physical labor is a natural antidepressant.
- Build at least one genuine human connection: an apprentice, an elder, a child.

## 3. Violence and Death Are Daily Life

You will witness: child death (infant mortality in early societies runs to double-digit
percentages), maternal death, brutal corporal punishment, the killing of tribal conflict.
**You cannot save everyone.**

- **Triage mindset**: spend limited emotional and medical resources on the savable.
  Staying composed is not coldness — if you break down, you can help no one.
- **Rehearse scripts**: before departure, write response scripts for extreme scenarios —
  "if my apprentice dies," "if my patron dies," "if I am expelled." People with scripts
  break down more slowly, because they are not improvising mid-trauma.

## 4. Pre-Departure Psychological Training Checklist

- [ ] 48 hours with no electronics and no entertainment (handwork and walking only)
- [ ] One week on a single-staple diet
- [ ] One camping trip without modern equipment (legal site, safety backup in place)
- [ ] One full night alone in the dark
- [ ] Write and rehearse three "worst-case scripts"
- [ ] Practice observer-perspective self-talk: replace "I'm finished" with
      "I notice that I am afraid"
