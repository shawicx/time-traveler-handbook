# Ancient Winter Survival

> Era guides (e.g. the [[xia-dynasty|Xia Dynasty]]) say "winter can starve or freeze you"
> without explaining what that means. This guide dissects every link of winter survival
> with **ancient materials + modern physics and medicine**.

## 1. Calibrate Your Sense of "Cold" First

A modern zero degrees: down jacket, a 15-minute commute, a 22°C interior.
An ancient sub-zero: **damp cold + wind chill + no heat replenishment all day +
a body on a calorie deficit**.

Two numbers to carve into memory:

1. **Water conducts heat ~25 times better than air** — wet clothing strips body heat far
   faster than intuition suggests. **Staying dry matters more than adding thickness.**
2. Wind constantly strips away the body-warmed air layer at your skin (wind chill) — with
   wind, the same air temperature can feel ten-plus degrees colder. **Wind-breaking is
   free insulation.**

## 2. The Physics of Warmth

Staying warm means **trapping still air**. From this, all practical rules follow:

- Multiple thin layers > one thick layer (the air *between* layers does the work).
- Loose-packed dry grass or reed fluff = cheap air layer — the down-fill of the ancient world.
- Any insulation system fails the moment it gets damp — moisture management runs through
  every section below.

## 3. Shelter

- **Semi-subterranean pit**: dig 1–1.5m down. Ground temperature is stable — winter soil
  is far warmer than the surface. This is what prehistoric peoples actually did; choose
  or help improve such housing after arrival.
- **The vent hole never closes**: a hearth indoors demands a smoke exit.
  **Carbon monoxide is colorless, odorless, and kills in sleep** — "smoky and warm" is not
  a good sign. This is knowledge locals lack and you can use to save an entire clan.
- **Entry**: open on the leeward side, double straw curtains (dry grass sandwiched between)
  forming an airlock.
- **The hearth**: center of the dwelling (maximizing radiant coverage). The key technique
  is **banking the fire**: bury the coals under ash overnight to smolder and preserve the
  fire, then uncover and breathe it back to flame in the morning. Fire management > daily
  fire-making.
- **The firewood ledger**: budget for "five winter months, burning day and night," completed
  before late autumn — this is a tonnage-scale calculation; foraging as you go will not
  survive the winter. Split and stack damp wood well ahead to dry.

## 4. Clothing and Bedding System

- **Three layers**: hemp inner layer (wicks sweat — swap and toast it dry by the fire
  whenever damp; **sweat-wet insides are more common than snow-wet outsides**) →
  middle layer **stuffed with dry grass / reed fluff / hemp tow** (sewn between two panels
  of cloth or hide — the "straw quilt/vest"; refreshing the stuffing restores performance)
  → hide outer layer (fur inward against the body is warmer; fur outward in snowfall to
  shed drift).
- **Feet first**: frostbite starts with wet feet. Bind puttees tight against wind;
  wrap sedge or dry grass around the feet as insoles — **swap for fresh dry grass the
  moment you come indoors**; keep a dedicated dry-grass pile by the door for feet and bedding.
- **Head and neck**: among the body's main idle-state heat-loss ports — a hide cap or head
  wrap outranks most people's intuition.
- **Sleep**: shared bedding for shared body heat (the historical norm — survival, not
  intimacy); eat something hot before sleep (diet-induced thermogenesis), tuck head and
  neck into the straw quilt while keeping breathing ventilated.

## 5. Food and Calories

- Basal metabolism rises in winter; stock food at "minimum survival ration +30%" redundancy.
- Calorie density ranking: **fat > dried meat > grain > dried greens**. Concentrate
  high-fat acquisition in autumn (the fat season of fishing and hunting, oil-rich gathers);
  spend the fat first in winter.
- Hot soup directly adds body heat — cooking at the hearth pays twice.
- **The scurvy line**: a months-long single-grain diet produces vitamin C deficiency
  (locals do not know the cause; you do). Stock dried greens, jujubes, and berries before
  winter; eat some every other day.

## 6. Behavioral Strategy

- Concentrate chores (water, firewood) in the warmest daylight window; melted snow and
  chiseled ice **must still be boiled**.
- Control exertion to **avoid heavy sweating** — sweat-dampened clothes become that night's
  heat drain. "Take off a layer before the long walk" is counterintuitive and correct.

## 7. Frostbite and Hypothermia (Modern First Aid)

### Frostbite

- Identify: red, swollen, stinging (first degree) → blisters (second) → waxy/blackened
  tissue (necrosis).
- Treat: re-warm slowly in **37–40°C water** (test with your elbow); keep dry and warm after.
- **Three prohibitions**: no snow-rubbing, no massage, no roasting by fire — mechanical and
  thermal shock worsen tissue death. Do not self-excise necrotic tissue.

### Hypothermia

- Identify: violent shivering (mild) → **shivering stops + confusion + paradoxical
  undressing urge (severe, immediately life-threatening)**.
- Treat: dry clothing → hot drinks if fully conscious → skin-to-skin heat (torso first) →
  continuous monitoring.
- **Two prohibitions**: no hot-water soaking of limbs (cold blood returning to the core
  causes central collapse); no alcohol ("drinking warms you" is a lethal error —
  vasodilation accelerates heat loss).

## 8. Pre-Departure Memory Checklist

- [ ] Carved in: **dry > warm** — wet clothing is winter's #1 killer
- [ ] Know how to bank a fire (coals under ash)
- [ ] Remember carbon monoxide: **the vent never closes**
- [ ] Remember the frostbite three prohibitions: no snow-rub / no massage / no fire-roast
- [ ] Remember the hypothermia red alert: **shivering stops**
- [ ] Remember the scurvy line: stock vitamin-C sources before winter
- [ ] For hearth-hut destinations: practice reading smoke exits and ventilation paths
