> This handbook follows one core premise: **the traveler arrives empty-handed — only
> their own body and the knowledge in their head.** This page tells you how to survive
> on that alone.
>
> The Spring and Autumn period is "the twilight of the aristocracy": the king a
> figurehead, hegemons rising in his name, warfare still bound by chivalric rules,
> diplomacy conducted by reciting poetry. It is comparatively gentle to wanderers —
> **every state competes for population, so you are welcome by default** — but the
> ladder upward is welded shut by hereditary ministerial houses. This page follows the
> [[zhou-dynasty|Zhou Dynasty]] overview and precedes the [[warring-states-period|Warring States]].

## 1. Era Snapshot

| Aspect | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| Time | c. 770 – 476 BC (King Ping's eastward move → the eve of Jin's partition) |
| Nominal order | The Zhou king remains overlord; real power sits with the hegemons |
| Region | Yellow River and Yangtze basins; a hundred-odd states bound by covenant networks |
| Polity | Hegemon politics: the strong lords convene covenants, "honor the king, repel the barbarians" |
| Economy | Well-fields cracking (Lu's first tax on mu, 594 BC); iron germinating; hollow-handled spade coins appear (late) |
| Writing | Great-seal script; the red-and-black inked Houma covenant texts |
| War | Aristocratic chariot combat, limited scale, bound by martial decorum |

King Ping moved east to Luoyi in 770 BC; the king's own campaign lost at Xuge in 707 BC finished royal prestige. Duke Huan of Qi took first hegemony (Kuiqiu covenant, 651 BC); Jin and Chu contended (Chengpu 632, Bi 597); the 546 BC peace conference split hegemony between Jin and Chu; Wu and Yue rose in turn (Yue destroyed Wu, 473 BC). The era closes amid collapsing rites, with Jin's partition already brewing — for the last three decades, read the [[warring-states-period|Warring States]] page alongside this one.

## 2. Before You Go

- **This is the aristocracy's twilight, not the commoner's dawn**: war, diplomacy, and sacrifice are a noble club; commoners are audience and logistics.
- **War still has rules**: no attacking unformed ranks, no wounding the fallen, no capturing the grey-haired — though they are breaking (at Hongshui in 638 BC, Duke Xiang of Song's decorum made him a laughingstock).
- **Population is state power**: every state recruits migrants to open land. **You are welcome by default** — the sharpest contrast with the Warring States and the Qin.
- **The ladder is nearly welded shut**: hereditary ministers rule. But Confucius is opening private schooling (551–479 BC) — the gate of "learning for office" has just cracked.
- **Medicine and record-keeping are professionalizing**: the physicians Huan (581 BC) and He (541 BC) show healing is now a craft separate from divination.
- **You have nothing**: the premise is unchanged — you arrive with only your body and your mind.

## 3. First 24 Hours

1. **Locate state and year**: ask "who holds the hegemony now?" — a memorized hegemon timeline dates you instantly (section 16). The state matters: Qi and Jin are rich, Zheng and Wei have vigorous trades, Chu and Qin are scorned as "barbarian" but rising, Song and Lu are proper but weak.
2. **Water first**: the eternal rule — **you must boil it**.
3. **Clothing**: jacket-and-skirt, hair bound up, lapel folded right — acquire local hemp dress fast. "Loose hair and left lapel" means barbarian to Zhou eyes; hairdo and lapel are identity.
4. **Attach to a settlement**: population is scarce; present yourself as "a refugee from the wars" and the steward will likely take you in — you are tax base and manpower.
5. **Do not discuss your origin**: "my border village was raided by troops or the Rong" suffices. No one checks closely — migration is constant in this age.

## 4. First 30 Days

- **Learn the standard speech and the local tongue**: yayan runs above the shi class. Month one: understand commands, recite your story.
- **Master a trade**: farming (everyone needs hands), crafts (Zheng and Wei are noted), commerce (Zheng's merchants covenant with their ruler — unusual standing).
- **Map the ministerial houses**: the danger is not the duke but the great lineages — the eve of "retainers ruling through their masters." Never bind yourself to a single house.
- **Prepare for winter**: unchanged; see [Ancient Winter Survival](/en/guides/winter-survival/).
- **Position as "descendant of a declined shi family"**: aristocratic society only recognizes the shi tier. Knowing rites and medicine and being able to recite the Odes makes you a perfect impoverished gentleman — far more useful than a plain commoner identity.

## 5. Identity & Social Status

| Tier | Role | Your position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Son of Heaven | The Zhou king, figurehead | Untouchable |
| Lords | Dukes through barons; hegemons emerge among them | Choose whom to follow |
| Ministerial houses | Hereditary lineages holding real power | The true ceiling |
| Shi | Lowest nobility, letters and arms | **Your assumed identity** |
| Guoren | City commoners with a tradition of political voice | Your actual start |
| Yeren | Rural cultivators | Bearers of tax and labor |
| Slaves | Bondmen and captives | Simply avoid |

**Guoren activism is the signature of the age**: collective city opinion ("all the guoren say kill him") can drive policy and depose rulers. As a city commoner you hold a minimal political shield. Upward mobility is rare, but three cracks exist: **military merit**, **learning** (private schools budding), and **retainership** (the embryo of the patron system).

## 6. Language & Communication

- **Language**: yayan above the shi; local dialects diverge widely. Local speech first, yayan second.
- **Writing**: great-seal script, still a monopoly of scribes, diviners, and recorders. Literacy is your most valuable "noble skill."
- **Reciting the Odes**: diplomacy and feasting quote the Songs — your memorized lines are the fastest social lubricant and the proof of your "gentleman" persona.
- **Numbers**: counting rods are standard; your mental arithmetic is instant competence.
- **Key vocabulary**: water, fire, food, person, come, go, good, bad — the standing list.

## 7. Food & Water

| Category | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Staple | Millet dominant; wheat and beans spreading; rice in the south |
| Commoner fare | Bean stew and greens, millet gruel, pickles; meat is rare |
| Cooking | Steaming, boiling, roasting, pickling — **no stir-frying** (the iron wok arrives in the Song) |
| Drink | Alcohol ritually measured, though aristocratic indulgence revives |
| Water | **Must be boiled** |

**Key survival knowledge**: famine years still kill. Identifying edible wild greens (shepherd's purse, goosefoot, bracken) is your lifeline — field marks and the edibility trial procedure are in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).

## 8. Clothing & Housing

- **Clothing**: jacket and skirt, hair bound, right lapel; hemp for most, silk for the few. The one-piece shenyi is emerging.
- **Headgear**: caps for the shi and above, kerchiefs for commoners. Your "gentleman" persona needs a decent cap — earn one.
- **Housing**: walled towns; rammed-earth halls for lords; semi-subterranean or thatched homes for the rest. Live inside a walled yi — not alone in the wild (Rong, Di, and bandits).
- **Bedding**: mats on the floor. Lice and fleas as ever — accept them.

## 9. Health & Medicine

**The witch and the physician are separating — you land exactly on that line.**

- **Speak in "six qi"**: the physician He already explains illness by yin, yang, wind, rain, dusk, and dawn. Explain your disinfection and isolation in that vocabulary — vastly more effective than "germs."
- **Hygiene is still miraculous**: boiling water, cleaning wounds, isolating the sick. Cities are small, so epidemic value is below the Warring States — but in camps and towns it still works instantly.
- **Herbs, midwifery, dental**: the standing plan; identification marks in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).
- **Psychology**: relatively little gore in this age — human sacrifice long abolished, wars limited. Covenant blood-oaths and public punishments are the main shocks; demands are lower than for the Shang or the Warring States, but still complete [Mental Preparation](/en/guides/mental-preparation/) first.

## 10. Safety & Risks

| Risk | Level | Response (knowledge only) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annexation (weak states fall) | Medium–high | **Avoid the crossroads battlegrounds** (Zheng, Song); land in the heart of Qi, Jin, Chu, or Qin |
| Entanglement in ministerial feuds | Medium | Watch half a year before attaching to a house |
| Wound infection | Lethal | Boiled-water rinse + clean dressing + isolation |
| Famine | Medium | Wild-plant identification; grain storage |
| Rong/Di raids (northern frontier) | Medium | Live inside walls; shelter with the townsfolk |
| Offending nobles | High | Keep the rites; never break persona |

**Female travelers**: courtship and remarriage are comparatively free, and noblewomen can exercise power — but in war, women are still the most immediate spoils. Attach to a great house and live by medicine or weaving. See [Female Traveler's Guide](/en/guides/female-traveler/).

## 11. Laws & Religion

- **Rites over law**: punishment hides in the offices — "if the penalties cannot be known, their awe cannot be measured." Zi Cheng's cast penal bronze (536 BC) breaks the tradition and shocks the realm.
- **Covenants are international law**: lords bind themselves by oath and divine curse; covenant-breaking invites collective punishment — if your state breaks faith, the war comes to you.
- **Beliefs**: ancestor cults and the altars of soil and grain; divination persists but no longer gates everything. **Human sacrifice is gone**; burial sacrifice lingers (Duke Mu of Qin, 177 retainers, 621 BC).
- **Martial decorum**: the rules of war are themselves law — envoys spared, unformed ranks unattacked — and they are dissolving. You need not obey them, but you must predict whether the other side will.
- **Your place**: healing and divination are legal; private schooling is dawning — the vocabulary of the "gentleman" (shi) is your best camouflage.

## 12. Economy & Trade

- **A currency in transition**: cowries fade; cloth and grain serve as equivalents; the hollow-handled spade coin appears in Jin lands late in the period.
- **Merchants stand fairly well**: Zheng's traders covenant with their ruler ("betray us not, and we shall not extort you"); Qi's fish-and-salt profits lead the world.
- **Salt remains hard currency**: your memorized salt geography still pays.
- **Your trading value**: medicine > literacy and record-keeping > agronomy > muscle. Hard currency: bronze chunks, cloth, salt, cowries.

## 13. Occupations & Skills

| Skill | Value | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medicine (modern knowledge) | Extremely high | You already have it |
| Literacy / scribe-diviner learning | High | Medium (great-seal script) |
| Trade (Zheng, Qi, Wei) | Medium–high | Medium |
| Farming | Medium | Low |
| Warrior (chariot combat) | Medium | High (nobles monopolize the training) |
| Craft (pottery, metal, weaving) | Medium | Medium |

**Optimal strategy**: stand on the twin identities of "declined gentleman + healer" — the persona gets you accepted by aristocratic society; the medicine gets you needed. Together they let you settle in any middling state.

## 14. Technology Level

- **Iron germinates**: Jin casts its penal vessel from iron in 513 BC; "fine metal for swords, coarse metal for hoes" — iron begins reaching the fields, but bronze still carries the load.
- **Ox-ploughing appears**: spreading late in the era (Confucius's disciples named "Ox" hint at it).
- **Bronze**: bell-chimes and ritual vessels exquisite; lost-wax casting present; weapons center on ge-halberds, ji-spears, and swords.
- **Military**: chariots with supporting infantry; Sun Wu presents his Art of War to King Helü of Wu (512 BC) — strategy becomes theory.
- **Astronomy**: court historians record eclipses continuously; intercalary rules refine.
- **Mathematics**: counting rods mature; practical surveying spreads.

## 15. Knowledge Advantage

The following is what you "know that Spring-Autumn people don't" — knowledge you can monetize from nothing:

- **Hygiene & disinfection**: boiling, wound care, isolation — delivered in "six-qi" vocabulary.
- **Agronomy**: seed selection, composting, ridge-farming — with iron scarce, these are the zero-cost yield engines.
- **Medicine & midwifery**: clean-hand delivery and boiled instruments — plummeting mortality is your reputation.
- **The Odes and the rites**: memorized lines and manners are your cheapest passport.
- **Astronomy**: recorders but no prediction system — your eclipse forecasts remain astonishing. Wrap them as "Heaven's warning; cultivate virtue" — never claim divinity.
- **Never flaunt**: military advice (it drags you into hegemon and ministerial struggles), iron-metallurgy improvements (state-run, and premature), political prophecy.
