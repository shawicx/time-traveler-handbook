> This handbook follows one core premise: **the traveler arrives empty-handed — only
> their own body and the knowledge in their head.** This page tells you how to survive
> on that alone.
>
> The Warring States is an age of "total war alongside the Hundred Schools": every
> order yields to farming and fighting, knowledge becomes valuable enough to trade for
> ministerships — and commoners are mobilized wholesale as war fuel for the first time.
> **Which year and which state you land in decide two completely different survival
> strategies.** This page follows the [[spring-and-autumn-period|Spring and Autumn]]
> and precedes the [[qin-dynasty|Qin Dynasty]].

## 1. Era Snapshot

| Aspect | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| Time | c. 475 – 221 BC (the Zhou royal house fell in 256 BC; after that, not even a nominal king) |
| Configuration | Seven powers: Qi, Chu, Yan, Han, Zhao, Wei, Qin |
| Polity | Reform centralization: commanderies and counties, merit ranks, household registration, bureaucratic documents |
| Economy | Iron ploughs and oxen universal; the Honggou canal network; four coinage systems — spade, knife, round, ant-nose |
| Writing | Five rival regional scripts; bamboo and silk books multiply explosively |
| War | Total war: infantry and cavalry, massed crossbows, hundreds of thousands in the field |
| Thought | The Hundred Schools contend — Confucian, Mohist, Daoist, Legalist, Logicians, Yin-Yang, Strategists, Agrarians… |

The partition of Jin (ennobled 403 BC) and the Tian usurpation in Qi (386) set the board; Wei reform first under Li Kui, Qin most thoroughly under Shang Yang (356); Guiling (354) and Maling (342) tilt Wei to Qi; decades of vertical and horizontal alliances; King Wuling of Zhao adopts nomad dress and cavalry archery (307); Yue Yi devastates Qi (284), Tian Dan restores it (279); at Changping (260) over 400,000 Zhao captives are buried alive; 230–221 BC, Qin swallows the six states one by one.

## 2. Before You Go

- **A head equals land**: Qin's merit ranks grade promotion by heads taken — war is this era's fastest elevator up, and fastest death.
- **The state wants all of you**: registration, mutual-liability units, conscription. Shang Yang's Qin is a precision war machine — landing in Qin and landing in Qi are two different worlds.
- **Knowledge has never been so valuable**: the Jixia Academy feeds scholars "who govern nothing but discuss everything"; Lord Mengchang keeps three thousand retainers; Su Qin wears six chancellor's seals. "Morning in Qin, evening in Chu" is not an insult — it is a career.
- **Iron and oxen feed the war**: the productivity dividend flows straight into total war. A bumper harvest means a bigger campaign next year.
- **State choice is destiny**: Qin (Legalist autocracy, merit society), Qi (a free academy, rich markets), Chu (vast, tradition-bound), Zhao (military innovation), Wei (exporter of talent) — memorize five playbooks before you land.
- **You have nothing**: the premise is unchanged — you arrive with only your body and your mind.

## 3. First 24 Hours

1. **Identify the state**: read the coinage (spade = Han/Zhao/Wei/Zhou lands, knife = Qi/Yan/Zhao, round = Qin/Zhou, ant-nose = Chu), the script (five regional variants), the army (Wei's heavy professional infantry, Zhao's light horse-archers, Qin's troops rejoicing at word of battle). Misidentify the state and every strategy fails.
2. **Water first**: the eternal rule — **you must boil it**.
3. **Clothing**: dress varies by state (Zhao already wears nomad fashion); acquire local garb fast.
4. **Attach**: head for a great city (Linzi, Handan, Daliang, Ying) and hire into a workshop or trading house; if your learning passes, go straight to a minister's gate as a retainer — **"a wandering scholar" is this era's most respectable opening line**, and no one checks genealogies.
5. **Do not discuss your origin**: "I have studied in all four directions and heard your lord welcomes scholars." Cross-border flow of shi is the norm — origins are the least questioned thing.

## 4. First 30 Days

- **Learn the standard speech and the local script**: scripts differ by state, so learn the one where you land; yayan remains the language of learning and diplomacy.
- **Pick your track**: technical (medicine, agronomy, craft — stable), scholarly (gentleman-scholar, debater — glorious but perilous), military (merit — fast and fatal). Your modern knowledge works on all three; **start technical, graduate to scholarly**.
- **Stay off the levy rolls**: every registered adult male is a conscript. Healers, artisans, and scholars have room for exemption — make yourself irreplaceable quickly.
- **Prepare for winter**: unchanged; see [Ancient Winter Survival](/en/guides/winter-survival/).
- **Position as "wandering scholar + healer"**: in the Warring States, knowledge is status. Literate, medical, conversant in cosmology — you are a shi by birthright of the mind.

## 5. Identity & Social Status

| Tier | Role | Your position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kings | Lords usurping the royal title (King Hui of Wei first, 344 BC) | Untouchable |
| Enfeoffed lords & ministers | Commoners turned chancellor: Su Qin, Fan Ju, Cai Ze, Lü Buwei | **Your theoretical ceiling** |
| Shi | Split into scholars, warriors, and debaters | **Your starting identity** |
| Registered commoners | Farmers, artisans, traders on the rolls | Most people's fate |
| Convicts & bondspeople | Criminal laborers, debt slaves | Never fall in |

**The era's greatest invention is self-made status**: birth no longer fixes the destination — Bai Gui trades to riches, Bian Que heals to fame, Su Qin wears six seals, Lin Xiangru leaps from a eunuch's retainer to chief minister. All your modern knowledge can be priced here; the price of failure is also unprecedented (Shang Yang torn by chariots, Wu Qi shot over his king's corpse, Su Qin torn by chariots).

## 6. Language & Communication

- **Yayan** remains the lingua franca of learning, diplomacy, and persuasion; the countryside still "speaks with different sounds."
- **Five script systems** (Qi, Chu, Yan, the three Jin, Qin) — your modern characters share roots but not shapes. **Learn the script of your landing state**; Qin's script has the greatest future payoff (it wins the 221 BC unification of writing).
- **Tools**: brush and bamboo slips; silk books appear in Chu. The literate are fought over by offices, ministers' gates, and merchant houses.
- **Debate is your gift**: the Logicians' "a white horse is not a horse," the strategists' morning-in-Qin-evening-in-Chu — a modern education's logic training makes you a competent debater by default. But debate is double-edged: one wrong sentence can cost your head.
- **Key vocabulary**: water, fire, food, person, come, go, good, bad — the standing list.

## 7. Food & Water

| Category | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Staple | Millet, rice, wheat, beans side by side; yields jump under iron and oxen |
| Side | Pork, dog, chicken, fish; sauces, meat pastes, and pickles richer than before |
| Cooking | Steaming, boiling, roasting, pickling — **no stir-frying** (the iron wok arrives in the Song) |
| Drink | Brewing is common and feasting endemic |
| Water | **Must be boiled** — big-city sources are fouler than ever |

**Key survival knowledge**: dense markets breed dense epidemics. Wild-plant identification remains your famine lifeline — field marks in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).

## 8. Clothing & Housing

- **Clothing**: the shenyi robe prevails, with regional variants (Zhao already in nomad dress); hemp for most, silk for the few. **A jade pendant and a sword are the shi's standard kit** — your persona needs both; earn them with medical fees.
- **Housing**: cities of a hundred thousand and more — Linzi with seventy thousand households, where "hubs strike hubs, shoulders rub shoulders, joined sleeves make tents, raised sleeves make canopies, shaken sweat falls like rain." Ward system intact; live inside a ward.
- **Architecture**: tall terraced towers, widespread eave tiles, ever-thicker rammed-earth walls.
- **Bedding**: floor mats, with low beds spreading. Lice and fleas as ever — accept them.

## 9. Health & Medicine

**Your rival is now theorized early medicine — your edge still dominates, and for the first time you hold a legal professional identity.**

- **Bian Que's four diagnostics** (observation, listening, questioning, pulse-taking) and his "six untreatables" — one of which is "trusting the shaman over the physician" — **medicine has publicly divorced witchcraft**. Practicing medicine is now legitimate.
- **Battlefield first aid is hard currency**: total war produces wounded by the ten thousand. Pressure hemostasis, splinting, wound cleaning, burn care — every army lacks such people. It is the fastest ladder and the rope tying you to the war machine.
- **Urban epidemics**: Linzi- and Handan-scale density is a plague hothouse. Your epidemiology (isolation, source protection, latrine management) finally has a real stage.
- **Herbs, midwifery, dental**: the standing plan; identification marks in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).
- **Psychology**: mass burials, city massacres, and extinction of states are this era's ambient noise. After Changping, "no one under Heaven gladly becomes Qin's subject." Desensitize yourself to mass-casualty scenes before departure ([Mental Preparation](/en/guides/mental-preparation/)).

## 10. Safety & Risks

| Risk | Level | Response (knowledge only) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conscription (total war) | High | Healer/artisan/scholar status seeks exemption; if conscripted, first-aid skills keep you alive |
| Collective-liability law (Qin-style states) | High | Extreme caution in Qin: never hide from rolls, never debate the law, never brawl |
| Wars of annihilation (230–221 BC) | Lethal | During the unification wars avoid highways, chokepoints, and capitals; shelter in the Qi or Chu heartlands |
| Ministerial purges (reform backlashes) | High | **Be the technical counselor, never the power-broker** — the cautionary list is long (Shang Yang, Wu Qi) |
| Urban epidemics | High | Boiling, isolation, water-source protection |
| Wound infection | Lethal | Boiled-water rinse + clean dressing |
| Famine | Medium | Wild-plant identification, grain storage |

**Female travelers**: women trade, head households, and practice medicine (attested in the Qin slips); medicine, weaving, and commerce are all viable — but war and pillage run hotter than in the Spring-Autumn. Anchor to a great city and a minister's gate; live by medicine and trade. See [Female Traveler's Guide](/en/guides/female-traveler/).

## 11. Laws & Religion

- **Written law everywhere**: Li Kui's Canon of Laws is the template, Qin's statutes the densest; mutual-liability units and heavy punishment for light faults (worst in Qin-style states).
- **Rites yield to force**: "the present age contends in strength" is the consensus. The old rites collapse; the new codes are cold — an age of contracts and punishments.
- **Beliefs**: the Five Virtues cycle theory rises (Zou Yan); states compete in sacrificing to mountains, rivers, and high gods; folk shamanism persists. **Human sacrifice is extinct**; burial sacrifice lingers (twenty-one women in the Zeng Hou Yi tomb).
- **Your place**: amid the Hundred Schools you may even found your own — with two iron rules: **never propose institutional change in a Legalist state**, and **never predict who wins the realm** (uncanny speech = death).
- **Persuasion red lines**: never touch a patron's fall or death (knives are cheap); never leak a served state's secrets (traitors are hunted by all).

## 12. Economy & Trade

- **Four coinage systems**: spade money (the three Jin and Zhou lands), knife money (Qi, Yan, Zhao), round coins (Qin, Zhou), ant-nose coins and Ying yuan gold plates (Chu) — **memorize the coins and their alloys; on arrival you can date the state and spot counterfeits**.
- **The rise of great private enterprise**: Yi Dun builds a fortune on salt, Guo Zong on iron, Bai Gui on "take what others discard, discard what others take" — private industry and commerce can rival states for the first time.
- **Hydraulics is state power**: the Honggou links the Yellow and Huai; Dujiangyan (256 BC) makes the Chengdu plain; the Zheng Guo canal (246 BC) enriches Guanzhong — hydraulic knowledge is a strategic asset every state covets.
- **Salt and iron remain lifelines**: Qi's salt, Zhao's iron, Chu's gold — state and private side by side. Your salt geography and metallurgy are employable, **but iron is strategic materiel: private smelting in Qin territory is death**.
- **Your trading value**: medicine > agronomy and hydraulics > literacy and paperwork > commercial technique > muscle. Hard currency: gold (Ying yuan), coins, grain.

## 13. Occupations & Skills

| Skill | Value | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scholar (a Jixia post, "governing nothing, discussing everything") | Extremely high (Qi is the best host) | Medium (your knowledge qualifies you) |
| Medicine (modern knowledge + battlefield first aid) | Extremely high | You already have it |
| Agronomy / hydraulics (reform-grade state assets) | High | Medium |
| Arms (merit ranks, generals' staffs) | High | High-risk |
| Trade (goods arbitrage) | Medium–high | Medium |
| Craft (smelting, weaving, lacquer) | Medium–high (mostly state-run) | Medium |
| Persuader / strategist | Extremely high | Extremely high, and extremely dangerous |

**Optimal strategy**: be a **technical counselor** in medicine and agronomy — needed, provisioned, never purged. The Jixia Academy in Qi is the safest perch (free speech, good pay, no questions about birth); Qin pays best but holds tightest. **Be Lü Buwei's retainer, not Shang Yang's reform partner.**

## 14. Technology Level

- **Iron**: cast-iron technique keeps improving, iron farm tools universal; by the late period, malleable cast iron is workable.
- **Agriculture**: iron plough and ox as standard; refined ridging and manuring; the Dujiangyan, Zheng Guo, and Honggou water systems.
- **Military**: crossbows (leg- and waist-spanned) in formation; infantry-cavalry mix; long ge, ji halberds, iron swords; every state walls its frontiers.
- **Bronze**: the Zeng Hou Yi bells (433 BC), two tones per bell; lost-wax casting at its height (the Zeng Hou Yi zun-pan).
- **Scholarship**: the Mozi Canon records the pinhole image and lever principles; Gan De and Shi Shen compile star catalogs (among the world's earliest); the 28 lunar mansions complete.
- **Textiles**: Chu's patterned silks and lacquer at their peak; glass eye-beads imported then imitated.
- **Compilation**: the Master Lü's Spring and Autumn Annals (239 BC) — three thousand retainers producing knowledge at scale.

## 15. Knowledge Advantage

The following is what you "know that Warring States people don't" — knowledge you can monetize from nothing:

- **Sanitation & epidemiology**: isolation, source protection, latrine management — the great cities finally give epidemiology its stage.
- **Agronomy**: rotation, composting, green manure, seed selection — "exhaust the land's strength" teaching doubled again; you are every reforming ruler's dream.
- **Hydraulic principles**: flow division, sediment flushing, bend hydraulics, silt fertilization — Dujiangyan-grade wisdom a century early.
- **Battlefield first aid**: hemostasis, splinting, debridement, burn care — a scarce resource in total war.
- **Mathematics & logic**: rod-arithmetic speed, geometric surveying, formal logic — offices want you, the Logicians respect you.
- **Astronomy & calendrics**: after Gan and Shi your edge is precision, not monopoly — collaborate as a calendrist; never duel.
- **Never flaunt**: crossbow or smelting improvements (war materiel — capital), predicting the realm's owner (uncanny speech — death), Legalist power-play performances (the fastest flame-outs; your cautionary list is long).
