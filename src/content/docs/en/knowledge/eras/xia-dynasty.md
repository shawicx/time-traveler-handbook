> This handbook follows one core premise: **the traveler arrives empty-handed — only
> their own body and the knowledge in their head.** This page tells you how to survive
> on that alone.
>
> The historicity of the Xia is still debated (whether the Erlitou culture *is* the Xia
> remains an open question). Travelers should treat it as shorthand for "early Central
> Plains bronze civilization" rather than a precise later-style dynasty.

## 1. Era Snapshot

| Aspect | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| Time | c. 2070 – 1600 BC |
| Region | Central Plains (modern western Henan, southern Shanxi) |
| Polity | Tribal alliance transitioning to early hereditary kingship |
| Economy | Subsistence agriculture + foraging; barter |
| Writing | No mature script; only carved marks |
| Metal | Early bronze, limited to small ritual items and tools |

Tradition holds the Xia was founded by Yu the Great; his son Qi succeeded him, beginning hereditary rule. Archaeologically it corresponds to the Erlitou culture (c. 1900–1500 BC), which has yielded large palace foundations and a bronze foundry, but **no written records**.

## 2. Before You Go

- **This is not "ancient China"**: no Chinese characters, no emperor, no coins, no iron. You land in a late-Neolithic tribal society.
- **You have nothing**: no clothes, no tools, no medicine, no salt. You arrive with only your body and the knowledge in your mind.
- **No written information**: records use knots and carved marks. Everything is oral and observational.
- **Seasons are life-or-death**: no food reserves; winter can starve you. If you can "choose" your arrival moment, aim for late spring or early summer.
- **Bronze is sacred**: what little bronze exists is rarer than gold in later eras — a symbol of kingship, not a daily utility.

## 3. First 24 Hours

1. **Orient yourself**: find a river (the Yi or Luo) and follow it — settlements exist only near water. Use the sun and stars to tell direction.
2. **Water first**: drinking river water raw causes parasitic infection. **You must boil it** — borrow pottery (common in any village) and an ember. No pottery? Filter through sand, or boil in a large bamboo section.
3. **Clothing**: wrap yourself in bark, vines, or hides as fast as possible. Winter exposure kills in hours.
4. **Attach to a clan**: going alone means death. Present yourself as a "refugee from afar" and ask to join a village. Your entry token is not an object — it is **immediately useful skill**: help them make fire, boil water, or treat a small wound.
5. **Do not discuss your origin**: any claim of "I'm from the future" is taken as sorcery and may get you executed. You are simply "a strange outsider who knows some healing."

## 4. First 30 Days

- **Learn the language**: Old Chinese is not mutually intelligible with Modern Chinese, but basic words (water, fire, food, person) can be picked up. Your month-one goal is to understand commands.
- **Master a craft**: pottery, weaving, and hunting have the lowest entry barriers. A skill earns your meals.
- **Observe power**: identify the clan chief, the priest, and the craft head. Their favor decides your survival.
- **Prepare for winter**: by autumn you must stockpile millet and dried meat. Central Plains winters drop below freezing.
- **Build a "healer" persona**: use hygiene knowledge (boiling water, isolating the sick, cleaning wounds) to pull off a few "miracle" cures and make yourself an irreplaceable religious figure.

## 5. Identity & Social Status

| Tier | Role | Your position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| King | Yu and his descendants; military and ritual leader | Untouchable |
| Nobles | Clan heads holding local power | Must be pleased |
| Commoners | Farmers, hunters, potters | Your starting identity |
| Slaves | Mostly war captives; lowest status | Avoid capture at all costs |

**There is no "free traveler" option.** In Xia society everyone must belong to a clan — otherwise you are treated as a bandit or spy. Your goal is to rise to "protected healer/craftsman," not to climb to noble.

## 6. Language & Communication

- **Language**: Old Chinese (Proto-Sino-Tibetan); tonal systems not yet formed, but grammar differs greatly from modern.
- **Writing**: **does not exist.** Do not attempt to write — no one can read.
- **Gestures**: the most reliable cross-linguistic tool. Pointing, miming, finger-counting.
- **Key vocabulary** (reconstructed, exact pronunciation unverifiable): water, fire, food, person, come, go, good, bad.
- **Numbers**: count on fingers and toes; most tribes grasp one to twenty.

## 7. Food & Water

| Category | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Staple | Millet — the absolute northern staple |
| Side | Wild greens, legumes, limited meat (pig, dog, deer) |
| Cooking | Boiled in pottery tripods; **no stir-frying** (the iron wok arrives in the Song) |
| Drinking | **Must be boiled**, or parasite infection follows |
| Foraging | Identifying edible plants is your survival skill — see "Knowledge Advantage" |

**Key survival knowledge**: before departure, memorize the distinction between common edible wild greens of the Central Plains (shepherd's purse, goosefoot, bracken) and poisonous lookalikes. This is not "bringing things" — it is bringing your mind. Knowing dozens of plants lets you outlive 90% of locals.

## 8. Clothing & Housing

- **Clothing**: you arrive with nothing — improvise from hemp bark, tree bark, hides. Learning to stitch simple garments with bone needles (villages have them) is a month-one task.
- **Shoes**: straw weave or barefoot. Winter demands hide wrappings.
- **Housing**: semi-subterranean pits with thatch, or surface wattle-and-daub huts. Lodge with a village; do not camp alone.
- **Bedding**: straw or hides on the ground; no beds. Lice and fleas are normal — accept them.

## 9. Health & Medicine

**This is your greatest edge — not from pills, but from knowledge.**

- **Disease is the #1 killer**: the Xia have no medicine at all. A minor infected wound is fatal — because they do not understand cleanliness.
- **Hygiene is a miracle**: boiling drinking water, washing wounds with boiled water, isolating the sick, burning the dead. These "common sense" practices slash mortality and will make you revered as a healer.
- **Wound care**: rinse with boiled water, wrap in clean (ideally heat-sterilized) hemp cloth. Avoid feces and soil contact.
- **Herbs you can use**: before departure, memorize common medicinal plants of the Central Plains — mugwort (staunch bleeding, repel insects), plantain (anti-diarrheal), dandelion (cooling). This is your "mind-carried pharmacy."
- **Childbirth**: maternal mortality is extremely high. If you know modern midwifery principles (washing hands, cutting the cord with a boiled stone blade, clean dressing), this is a huge bargaining chip.
- **Dental**: no dentists. Clean teeth by chewing willow twigs to a brush (an ancient method); avoid cracking them on hard objects.

## 10. Safety & Risks

| Risk | Level | Response (knowledge only) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wound infection | Lethal | Boiled-water rinse + clean dressing + isolation |
| Tribal conflict | High | Learn the spear; never travel alone; ally with the strong |
| Famine | High | Identify edible plants; store grain; learn fishing/trapping |
| Wildlife | Medium | Travel in groups; carry torches; identify snakes |
| Enslavement | Medium | Guard your clan identity; avoid borders |
| Offending nobles/priests | Lethal | Strictly obey ritual; do not speak rashly |

## 11. Laws & Religion

- **Law**: no written code. Disputes are settled by the clan chief; punishments are brutal (corporal, exile, execution).
- **Beliefs**: animism and ancestor worship are central. The highest deity is "Heaven" or "Shangdi" (not the Christian sense).
- **Ritual**: the most sacred activity. Profaning a rite or an ancestral spirit tablet means exile at best, execution at worst.
- **Taboos**: never stare at the king's rites; never touch ritual vessels (bronze ding, jade cong).
- **Your place**: the role of "shaman-healer" fits the Xia belief system perfectly — they need a "sky-toucher" like you.

## 12. Economy & Trade

- **No currency**: exchange is by barter. **Cowrie shells** (from distant coasts) are among the few items with general-equivalent status.
- **Hard currency**: salt, small bronzes, jades, cowries. You cannot conjure these, but you can earn them through service.
- **Your trading value**: your labor, healing, and farming advice are tradable services. Curing the chief is worth more than any object.
- **Sourcing salt**: salt is scarce. If you can locate saline soil or a salt spring (memorize Central Plains salt geology before departure) and teach the clan to boil brine, you become indispensable.

## 13. Occupations & Skills

| Skill | Value | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pottery | High (essential) | Medium |
| Weaving (hemp, straw) | High | Low |
| Hunting | Medium (risky) | Medium |
| Farming (millet) | Medium | Low |
| Bronze casting | Extremely high (scarce) | Extremely high (apprenticeship required) |
| Medicine (modern knowledge) | Extremely high | You already have it |

**Optimal strategy**: establish yourself as an "exiled healer." Modern medical knowledge makes you a scarce, protected, irreplaceable resource. Back it up with a basic craft (pottery or weaving) for daily meals.

## 14. Technology Level

- **Bronze**: just emerging; only small items (knives, awls, mirrors, ritual vessels). **Casting is a state secret.**
- **Iron**: does not exist.
- **Pottery**: grey and black wares are refined; wheel-throwing is widespread. The easiest craft for you to learn.
- **Jade**: high-grade jades (gui tablets, zhang scepters) are central to noble status and ritual.
- **Agriculture**: millet is the staple, harvested with stone sickles and bone spades. Rice appears in the south but is rare on the Central Plains.
- **Transport**: on foot or dugout canoe. No horse-drawn carts (those arrive in the Shang).
- **Records**: knots and carved marks. No writing.

## 15. Knowledge Advantage

The following is what you "know that Xia people don't" — knowledge you can monetize from nothing:

- **Hygiene & disinfection**: boiling water, wound cleanliness, isolating the sick, burning corpses. This immediately lowers mortality and earns you the reputation of a healer.
- **Basic agriculture**: crop rotation, composting, seed selection. Can double a village's millet yield.
- **Simple engineering**: irrigation channels, drying racks, improved kilns. Buildable with the wood-and-stone tools the Xia already have.
- **Astronomy**: the cyclical nature of eclipses reinforces your image as one who "communicates with Heaven."
- **Mapping & orientation**: star-based navigation and reading terrain for water far outperform locals.
- **Herbal identification**: memorizing dozens of medicinal plants is equivalent to carrying a pharmacy — in your head.

**Knowledge you must NOT flaunt**: metallurgy (triggers war), gunpowder (useless and dangerous), political ideals (offend the powerful).

## 16. Knowledge & Skills to Memorize Before Departure (Checklist)

This is not a packing list — you can bring nothing. These are the **knowledge and skills you must load into your mind** before you go:

**Medical knowledge**
- [ ] Memorize: all drinking water must be boiled; all wounds must be cleaned with boiled water
- [ ] Know the form and use of 10+ common medicinal plants (mugwort, plantain, dandelion, honeysuckle…)
- [ ] Master basic delivery and cord-care (clean hands + a boiled cutting tool)
- [ ] Know fracture-fixation principles (splints, traction)

**Geography & orientation**
- [ ] Memorize the map of Erlitou / the Yi-Luo river basin
- [ ] Memorize the location of major Central Plains salt sources (e.g. the Xiezhou salt lake)
- [ ] Learn star-based navigation (Polaris, the Big Dipper)

**Agriculture & craft**
- [ ] Know the principles of crop rotation, composting, seed selection
- [ ] Learn cordage, basket-weaving, and friction-fire (bow-drill method)
- [ ] Know millet planting and storage essentials

**Language & society**
- [ ] Memorize basic Old Chinese vocabulary (even if pronunciation is off)
- [ ] Know the Xia social structure (king–noble–commoner–slave) and taboos
- [ ] Prepare your "exiled healer" persona and cover story
