> This handbook follows one core premise: **the traveler arrives empty-handed — only
> their own body and the knowledge in their head.** This page tells you how to survive
> on that alone.
>
> Unlike the [[xia-dynasty|Xia Dynasty]], the Shang is doubly attested — by oracle-bone
> inscriptions and by the excavated capital at Yinxu — making it the first "historic"
> Chinese dynasty. That changes nothing about your situation: you still arrive with
> nothing. The Shang's first lesson: this era has bronze at its zenith, cities in
> their infancy, and **institutionalized human sacrifice**.

## 1. Era Snapshot

| Aspect | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| Time | c. 1600 – 1046 BC |
| Region | Central Plains (core in modern northern Henan; late capital Yin = Anyang) |
| Polity | Sacred kingship; "inner and outer services" (royal domain + vassal lords) |
| Economy | Bronze-Age agriculture + royal craft workshops; cowries function as money |
| Writing | **Oracle-bone script** — mature Chinese writing, monopolized by diviners |
| Metal | Bronze casting at its peak: ritual vessels, weapons, chariot fittings |

The Shang was founded when Tang overthrew Jie of Xia. After repeated moves the court settled at Yin (Anyang) under Pan Geng, c. 1300 BC — hence "Yin" as the dynasty's other name. Under Wu Ding (c. 1250–1192 BC) the kingdom reached its height. The last king, Di Xin (Zhou), fell to King Wu of Zhou at the Battle of Muye, 1046 BC.

## 2. Before You Go

- **Writing exists, but not for you**: oracle-bone script is a divination archive; reading and carving belong to the "diviner" (zhenren) clique. Your knowledge of simplified characters is nearly useless here — yet your literacy in Chinese is a long-term asset.
- **Human sacrifice is an institution, not an accident**: royal rites consumed victims by the thousands (oracle bones record over ten thousand), mostly war captives (especially the Qiang). **Unexplained strangers are a high-risk group.**
- **Cities have appeared**: the late capital, the "Great Settlement Shang," held a population of tens of thousands, with bronze, bone, and pottery workshops. You can "enter the city" — the biggest improvement over the Xia.
- **Peak bronze has nothing to do with you**: ritual vessels and weapons belong to kingship and the cult. The crafts within your reach are pottery, bone, wood, and hemp.
- **Alcohol is the national pastime and national curse**: from kings to commoners the Shang drank heavily from jue, jia, and gu cups. Staying sober is a free cognitive edge.
- **You have nothing**: the premise is unchanged — you arrive with only your body and your mind.

## 3. First 24 Hours

1. **Locate yourself**: rammed-earth walls, ash pits, and foundry smoke mean you are in the Shang. Stay clear of the royal cemetery and sacrificial pits — that is where blood flows, not where you seek help.
2. **Water first**: drinking river water raw causes parasitic infection. **You must boil it** — borrow pottery and an ember; otherwise filter through sand or boil in a bamboo section. City wells are no safer raw.
3. **Clothing**: wrap yourself in hemp bark, tree bark, or hides immediately. More importantly, **make yourself look like a local** — in the Shang, an odd-looking stranger may be reported as a foreign captive.
4. **Attach to a lineage settlement**: present yourself as a "refugee from afar" and ask to join a lineage within the city. Your entry token remains **immediately useful skill** — mend a kiln, work bone, treat a small wound.
5. **Do not discuss your origin, and do not perform "miracles"**: the power to interpret omens belongs to the diviners. "Prophesying" on day one is a challenge to royal authority.

## 4. First 30 Days

- **Learn the language**: Shang speech is still Old Chinese, not mutually intelligible with Modern Chinese. Your month-one goal is to understand the foreman and voice your needs.
- **Master a craft**: bone-working and pottery have the lowest barriers and the highest demand (urban workshops are chronically short-handed). A skill earns your meals and your lineage's protection.
- **Observe power**: tell apart the king and royal clan, the diviners, the royal ladies, workshop overseers, and lineage heads. Whoever trusts you decides whether you live.
- **Prepare for winter**: Central Plains winters drop below freezing; by autumn you must stockpile millet and dried meat. For banked fires, straw quilts, and hypothermia first aid, see [Ancient Winter Survival](/en/guides/winter-survival/).
- **Position as "healer," not "shaman"**: Shang cult offices (diviner, medium, scribe) are professionalized and closed to outsiders. Establish yourself through medicine and craft; let results speak; never contest the clergy's interpretive monopoly.

## 5. Identity & Social Status

| Tier | Role | Your position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| King | The Shang king; supreme military and ritual leader | Untouchable |
| Royal clan & nobles | The royal lineages, royal consorts and princes | Must be pleased |
| Regional lords | Vassal chiefs of the "outer service" | Keep clear of their wars |
| Commoners | Farmers, artisans, herders | Your starting identity |
| Slaves & captives | Mostly war captives (Qiang and others); used as sacrificial victims | **The gravest threat to your survival** |

**There is no "free traveler" option.** Everyone belongs to a lineage; the lineage-less are treated as spies or runaway slaves. Your goal is "protected healer/craftsman of such-and-such lineage" — never to approach the royal house. The closer to the king, the closer to the sacrificial pit.

## 6. Language & Communication

- **Language**: Old Chinese; tones not yet fixed, grammar far from modern. Gestures, miming, and finger-counting remain your most reliable tools.
- **Writing**: **exists but monopolized**. Oracle-bone script has some 4,500 distinct characters, of which about a third are deciphered. Forms are largely pictographic and ideographic — with a background in traditional characters you will learn to read them far faster than any local.
- **Carving is forbidden ground**: incising an oracle bone impersonates a diviner — a capital offense. To learn the script, memorize by observation first.
- **The sexagenary cycle is in daily use**: days run on the sixty-day ganzhi cycle, and Shang kings' temple names use the heavenly stems (Wu Ding, Zu Jia, Di Xin). **Memorize the ganzhi table before departure** — someone who can "count the days" flawlessly will be thought to know Heaven's way.
- **Key vocabulary**: water, fire, food, person, come, go, good, bad (pronunciation unverifiable; memorize meanings first).

## 7. Food & Water

| Category | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Staple | Millet (setaria and broomcorn); wheat and rice spreading |
| Side | Pork, dog, beef, mutton, wild greens, legumes; plentiful for nobles, scarce for commoners |
| Cooking | Boiled in ding and li tripods, steamed in yan — **no stir-frying** (the iron wok arrives in the Song) |
| Drink | Sweet ale (li) and aromatic ale (chang) flow freely; **restraint is your edge** |
| Water | **Must be boiled** — city wells included |

**Key survival knowledge**: Shang food security rests on commoners' labor, and in famine years the bottom starves first. Identifying edible wild greens (shepherd's purse, goosefoot, bracken) remains your lifeline — field marks and the edibility trial procedure are in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).

## 8. Clothing & Housing

- **Clothing**: commoners wear hemp; nobles wear silk. You still improvise from bark and hides, stitching with bone needles. Replace them with local hemp garments quickly to shed the "alien" look.
- **Shoes**: straw weave or barefoot; hide wrappings in winter.
- **Housing**: rammed-earth platform houses for nobles, semi-subterranean dwellings for commoners. Seek lodging within a lineage quarter; do not live alone outside the walls.
- **Bedding**: straw and hides on the ground. Lice and fleas are normal — accept them.

## 9. Health & Medicine

**Your greatest edge is still knowledge, not pills.**

- **Illness is divined, death is probable**: the Shang consult the ancestors first; "treatment" means sacrifice. Your boiling, wound-cleaning, and isolation achieve what their rites cannot.
- **Strategy: borrow the sacred, practice medicine**: credit your cures to "proper sacrifice to the ancestors" and share the glory with the diviners. **Competing with the diviners for credit is fatal.**
- **Wound care**: rinse with boiled water; wrap in clean (ideally fire-scorched) hemp cloth. Avoid feces and soil contact.
- **Herbs**: mugwort (staunch bleeding, repel insects), plantain (anti-diarrheal), dandelion (cooling) — your "mind-carried pharmacy"; identification marks in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).
- **The psychological toll of blood**: you will witness human sacrifice and burial. Without prior desensitization, horror, vomiting, or collapse will expose you. Complete [Mental Preparation](/en/guides/mental-preparation/) before departure.
- **Alcohol**: heavy drinking is endemic; drunken brawls and alcohol poisoning are common. Abstain or sip minimally — it keeps you clear-headed and safe.
- **Childbirth & dental**: clean-hand midwifery with a boiled cutting tool, willow-twig tooth cleaning — as in the Xia, these are your core bargaining chips.

## 10. Safety & Risks

| Risk | Level | Response (knowledge only) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seized as sacrificial victim | **Lethal** | Blend into a lineage; conform in speech and manner; avoid rites and war zones |
| Military conscription | High | Feigning illness invites divination — instead, hold a protected artisan status |
| Wound infection | Lethal | Boiled-water rinse + clean dressing + isolation |
| Famine | High | Identify edible plants; store grain; learn fishing/trapping |
| Offending diviners/nobles | Lethal | Strict ritual obedience; credit cures to the ancestors |
| Drunken violence | Medium | Don't drink (or barely); avoid feasts |

**Female travelers**: Shang women of rank fared better than in most later eras (Fu Hao led armies and presided over rites) — but that was the privilege of royal women. An unattached woman faces sexual violence, forced marriage, and even sacrifice. See [Female Traveler's Guide](/en/guides/female-traveler/).

## 11. Laws & Religion

- **Law**: no written code. Judgments come from the king, lineage heads, and divination; punishments are brutal (foot amputation, nose cutting, execution).
- **Beliefs**: the supreme god is Di, master of rain, wind, and war; ancestor worship is the core — the king reaches his ancestors through sacrifice.
- **Divination precedes every decision**: sacrifice, war, hunts, births, illness — nothing is done unasked. Interpretation belongs to the diviner clique.
- **Human sacrifice & burial**: royal rites consume captives; royal funerals bury retainers. You cannot stop it — wear a practiced, unimpressed face.
- **Taboos**: never touch ritual vessels (bronze ding, jue) or oracle bones; never watch a divination uninvited; never comment on the king or the ancestors.
- **Your place**: enter as a "healer-artisan," never as a "spirit-medium." The Shang clergy's threshold and exclusivity far exceed the Xia's.

## 12. Economy & Trade

- **Proto-currency**: cowrie shells function as money, counted in peng; a royal gift of cowries is a supreme honor. Bronze cowrie imitations have appeared — the herald of metal coinage.
- **Hard currency**: cowries, salt, jade, small bronzes. You cannot conjure these, but skill and medicine can earn them.
- **Your trading value**: medicine > craft > muscle. Curing a lineage head outranks any object.
- **Salt**: still a strategic good. If you can locate saline soil or a salt spring (memorize Central Plains salt geology before departure) and teach brine-boiling, your standing in the lineage becomes unshakable.

## 13. Occupations & Skills

| Skill | Value | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bone-working (pins, awls, arrowheads) | High (urban demand) | Low |
| Pottery | High | Medium |
| Weaving (hemp, straw) | High | Low |
| Farming (millet) | Medium | Low |
| Horses & chariots | Extremely high (new & scarce) | High |
| Bronze casting | Extremely high | Extremely high (royal monopoly; needs patronage) |
| Script & carving | Extremely high | **Forbidden ground** (diviners' monopoly) |
| Medicine (modern knowledge) | Extremely high | You already have it |

**Optimal strategy**: enter the city as a bone-worker or potter to earn meals, build irreplaceability through medicine, and over the long term learn horses and chariots. Stay out of the inner bronze-casting circles — that is the heart of royal power; prying eyes are a crime.

## 14. Technology Level

- **Bronze**: piece-mold casting at its absolute peak — the Houmuwu ding exceeds 800 kg. Ritual vessels, weapons, chariot fittings are all covered. **Casting is a royal-workshop monopoly.**
- **Chariots**: the two-wheeled horse-drawn chariot transforms warfare (arriving around Wu Ding's time); two horses, three crew (driver, archer, halberdier).
- **Weapons**: ge halberds, spears, knives, axes, bows and arrows, bronze helmets.
- **Writing**: oracle-bone inscriptions; characters like "ce" (slips) and "dian" (codex) suggest bamboo books coexisted.
- **Calendar**: an unbroken sexagenary day-count plus the rudiments of a luni-solar calendar with intercalation.
- **Agriculture**: millet staple; stone, wood, and bone tools still do the fieldwork — bronze does not farm.
- **Crafts**: white pottery, proto-porcelain in its infancy, jade work, lacquer, silk.
- **Transport**: on foot, by boat, and by chariot (noble and military use).

## 15. Knowledge Advantage

The following is what you "know that Shang people don't" — knowledge you can monetize from nothing:

- **Hygiene & disinfection**: boiling water, wound cleanliness, isolating the sick. This immediately lowers mortality and earns you fame as a healer — remember to share the glory with the ancestors and diviners.
- **Basic agriculture**: crop rotation, composting, seed selection. Millet yields rise markedly.
- **Simple engineering**: irrigation channels, drying racks, improved kilns and workshop flow for bone-working.
- **Astronomy**: the cyclicity of eclipses. **Use sparingly** — one "prediction" builds your name; a second puts you on a collision course with the diviners.
- **Ganzhi & arithmetic**: a memorized sexagenary table and decimal mental math outperform everyone at counting days, rationing, and tallying — without touching the diviners' domain.
- **Mapping & orientation**: star navigation and reading terrain for water and salt.
- **Herbal identification**: dozens of medicinal plants equal a pharmacy in your head.

**Knowledge you must NOT flaunt**: metallurgy (a royal secret — leaking it means death), faked oracle divination (defrauding the cult — execution), military technology transfers (triggers campaigns), political ideals (offends the powerful).

## 16. Knowledge & Skills to Memorize Before Departure (Checklist)

This is not a packing list — you can bring nothing. These are the **knowledge and skills you must load into your mind** before you go:

**Medical knowledge**
- [ ] Memorize: all drinking water must be boiled; all wounds must be cleaned with boiled water
- [ ] Know the **field identification marks** and uses of 10+ common medicinal plants (mugwort, plantain, dandelion, honeysuckle…) — check each against the tables in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/)
- [ ] Master basic delivery and cord-care (clean hands + a boiled cutting tool)
- [ ] Know fracture-fixation principles (splints, traction)

**Geography & orientation**
- [ ] Memorize the rough locations of Anyang (Yinxu) and the Zhengzhou Shang city, and the rivers (the Huan, the Yellow River)
- [ ] Memorize the location of major Central Plains salt sources (e.g. the Xiezhou salt lake)
- [ ] Learn star-based navigation (Polaris, the Big Dipper)

**Agriculture & craft**
- [ ] Know the principles of crop rotation, composting, seed selection
- [ ] Learn cordage, basket-weaving, and friction-fire (bow-drill method) — procedure in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/)
- [ ] Know the basic workflows of bone-working and pottery (material selection, shaping, grinding / clay choice, forming, firing)

**Mind & special topics**
- [ ] Complete desensitization to gore (sacrifice, trauma) and worst-case rehearsal ([Mental Preparation](/en/guides/mental-preparation/))
- [ ] Remember the winter triad: banked fire, dry grass, stay dry ([Ancient Winter Survival](/en/guides/winter-survival/))
- [ ] Female travelers additionally read [Female Traveler's Guide](/en/guides/female-traveler/)

**Language & society**
- [ ] **Memorize the sixty-entry ganzhi (sexagenary) table** — Shang day-counting and royal temple names run on it
- [ ] Memorize basic Shang vocabulary (even if pronunciation is off) and a gesture-based communication plan
- [ ] Know the Shang social structure (king – royal clans – regional lords – commoners – slaves) and its taboos
- [ ] Prepare your "exiled healer-artisan" persona: **never claim to be a medium, never perform prophecies, never touch divination**
