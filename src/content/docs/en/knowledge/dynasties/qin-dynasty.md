> This handbook follows one core premise: **the traveler arrives empty-handed — only
> their own body and the knowledge in their head.** This page tells you how to survive
> on that alone.
>
> The Qin is China's first unified empire — and the **least friendly peacetime era**
> for travelers: the threat is no longer the Shang altar or the Warring States
> battlefield, but **the state machine itself** — registration, travel permits,
> collective liability, corvée. The unregistered cannot move an inch. The dynasty
> also lives only fifteen years (chaos from 209 BC), so **your arrival date nearly
> decides your fate**.

## 1. Era Snapshot

| Aspect | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| Time | 221 – 207 BC (the pre-unification state of Qin belongs to the [[zhou-dynasty|Zhou Dynasty]] page) |
| Region | The whole realm in commanderies and counties (from 36 upward); capital Xianyang |
| Polity | Imperial throne + Three Ministers and Nine Courts + commandery-county bureaucracy |
| Economy | State salt and iron monopolies; unified banliang coin and weights & measures; crushing tax and corvée |
| Writing | One script: small-seal for office use, Qin clerical (lishu) in daily practice |
| Engineering | Great Wall, express highways, the Straight Road, the Lingqu canal, Lishan and Epang — hundreds of thousands of convict laborers |

King Ying Zheng extinguished the six states in a decade (230–221 BC), styled himself "First Emperor," abolished enfeoffment for commanderies and counties, and unified script, coinage, weights, measures, and axle gauges. Books were burned in 213 BC and scholars buried in 212 BC; the Xiongnu were struck in the north and the Yue subdued in the south. The First Emperor died at Shaqiu in 210 BC; Chen Sheng and Wu Guang rose at Dazexiang in 209 BC and the six states revived; Ziying surrendered to Liu Bang in 207 BC (the Chu–Han contest then ran to 202 BC — see the future Han page).

## 2. Before You Go

- **The enemy is the state machine**: universal registration, five-and-ten household mutual liability, and the travel-permit system — without household registration and a permit no inn dares host you and no checkpoint lets you pass. Shang Yang himself was trapped by his own law while fleeing ("harboring a guest without papers is a crime").
- **A fifteen-year countdown**: 221–210 BC is the harsh but stable order; from 209 BC the realm burns. Your arrival year decides your whole strategy.
- **Books burn, but not medicine or farming**: the book-ban decree spared works on "medicine, divination, and planting" — **your entire knowledge domain happens to be legal**.
- **The script is already clerical**: daily documents run in Qin lishu (the Shuihudi slips prove it), close to the characters you know. Literacy has never been cheaper — the literate can enter government service.
- **Infrastructure without precedent**: imperial highways span the realm, the Lingqu canal links river systems, weights and measures are uniform — the official roads are fast and smooth, and the most tightly checked.
- **You have nothing**: the premise is unchanged — you arrive with only your body and your mind.

## 3. First 24 Hours

1. **Date your landing**: order period (documents galloping down the highways, banliang coins in the market, stern bailiffs) or collapse period (Chen Sheng's banners, Chu revivals, routed soldiers on the roads)? This decides "settle" versus "flee."
2. **Water first**: the rule is eternal — **you must boil it**.
3. **Clothing**: plain hemp, blend in fast. The Qin favors black officially, but commoners simply aim for unmarked plainness.
4. **The critical step — register, do not wander**: enter a "li" (walled village) immediately and report to the li head as "a subject who lost his registration in the wars." In Qin, **voluntary registration is the way to live; being caught unregistered is the way to die**.
5. **Do not discuss your origin**: conquest, forced relocations of the wealthy, and war created floods of displaced people — "a registration-less subject of a fallen state" is an unimpeachable cover.

## 4. First 30 Days

- **Learn the language and script**: official speech follows Qin pronunciation; practice clerical script — your modern characters descend from it, and practicing brush lishu is pre-departure homework.
- **Master a trade the government needs**: medicine, agronomy, craft. A unifying empire is digesting six states and its county offices always lack technicians.
- **Map your mutual-liability unit**: neighbors are legally obliged to inform on each other. Never complain about the new laws, never shelter runaways, never befriend loud critics.
- **Prepare for winter**: unchanged — stock millet and firewood by autumn; see [Ancient Winter Survival](/en/guides/winter-survival/).
- **Position as "the literate healer"**: medicine plus literacy — both government and people need you, and both are perfectly legal.

## 5. Identity & Social Status

| Tier | Role | Your position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emperor | The First Emperor and successors | Untouchable |
| Ministers & commandery officials | The bureaucracy | Your direct superiors |
| Ranked | Twenty grades of rank (military merit) | To be courted |
| Shiwu | Unranked commoners | **Your starting identity** |
| Convict laborers | Chengdan, chong, kun, qian — hundreds of thousands | **Never fall in** |
| Private slaves | Limited numbers | Simply avoid |

With unification, wars taper off and **the military-merit ladder all but closes** — technical skill (medicine, agronomy) and paperwork (scribe, registrar) are the two respectable channels left to a commoner.

## 6. Language & Communication

- **Language**: official speech is based on Qin pronunciation; the old six states still speak their own tongues. Learn the local speech first, then Qin official pronunciation.
- **Writing**: small-seal is the official face; daily documents run in clerical script. **Clerical characters are already close to the ones you know** — the lowest literacy barrier of any era in this handbook.
- **A documentary society**: property, disputes, and identity all live in "yuanshu" (official records). Anyone who writes clean, standard documents is wanted by every county office.
- **Numbers**: counting rods and decimal arithmetic are mature, and weights & measures are now uniform — your mental arithmetic is instant competence.
- **Key vocabulary**: water, fire, food, person, come, go, good, bad — the standing list.

## 7. Food & Water

| Category | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Staple | Millet, wheat, rice by region; weights & measures uniform realm-wide |
| Side | Green stews, beans, pickles; meat is rare |
| Cooking | Steaming, boiling, roasting, pickling — **no stir-frying** (the iron wok arrives in the Song) |
| Salt & iron | **State monopoly** — private boiling of brine or casting of iron is a grave crime |
| Water | **Must be boiled** — work sites and convict camps above all |

**Key survival knowledge**: conscript rations are meager and poor. Identifying edible wild greens (shepherd's purse, goosefoot, bracken) remains your lifeline — field marks in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).

## 8. Clothing & Housing

- **Clothing**: plain hemp (natural or black), unadorned and unmarked. Ranked dress is stratified — do not overstep.
- **Housing**: you must live inside a "li" — walled, gated, closed at dusk. **Anyone dwelling alone in the wilds is a bandit suspect**.
- **Cities**: Xianyang, swollen by 120,000 relocated wealthy households, is the realm's greatest city; the old six capitals keep their markets.
- **Bedding**: mats on the floor. Lice and fleas, as ever — accept them.

## 9. Health & Medicine

**For the first time, the state is on your side — at the price of becoming its technician.**

- **State medicine exists**: the Grand Physician directs medical affairs; **the "li-hostel" forcibly isolates lepers** (per the Shuihudi slips, among the world's earliest quarantine institutions); the Sealing-and-Examining forms already codify forensic examination. Your epidemiology aligns with state practice instead of being read as sorcery.
- **Convict camps are plague pits**: Lishan, the Wall, and the southern works pack people into sanitary collapse. If conscripted, boiling water, isolating the sick, and wound care are your entire survival capital.
- **Hygiene still pays**: only your identity shifts from "miracle healer" to "the healer the office cannot replace" — stable, but not glorious.
- **Herbs, midwifery, dental**: the standing plan; identification marks in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).
- **Psychology**: public execution (market display, bisection, clan extermination) is routine urban administration. A composed face at the sight is basic self-protection — desensitize beforehand ([Mental Preparation](/en/guides/mental-preparation/)).

## 10. Safety & Risks

| Risk | Level | Response (knowledge only) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrested unregistered into convict labor | **Lethal** | Report and register at a li on day one |
| Corvée levy (garrison, transport, works) | High | Irreplaceable healer/artisan status defers it |
| Collective liability & informing | High | Watch your words; fulfill the duty to inform, but never weaponize grudges |
| Harsh punishment for small faults | High | **Memorize the Qin legal red lines** (section 11) |
| End-of-dynasty war (after 209 BC) | Lethal | Once chaos is clear, avoid highways and the capital region; head for border commanderies or the southeast |
| Epidemics | Medium | Boiling, isolation, latrine hygiene — your home ground |
| Famine | Medium | Wild-plant identification, grain storage |

**Female travelers**: Qin law lets women head households and keep dowry property — legal standing is not low — but an unregistered single woman still cannot escape the registration net. Practice medicine or weaving and attach to a li household quickly. See [Female Traveler's Guide](/en/guides/female-traveler/).

## 11. Laws & Religion

- **Total Legalist rule**: "the law is the teaching, the clerk the teacher." Statutes on theft, banditry, custody, arrest, miscellany, and punishment are dense; mutilating and labor punishments dominate; clan extermination for the gravest crimes.
- **Red lines to memorize**: possessing banned books (the Odes, Documents, and school texts), discussing the Odes and Documents in public (market execution), "citing the past to fault the present" (clan extermination), slanderous or uncanny speech (death), private brawling (heavy punishment), private salt or iron (grave crime), sheltering runaways (collective liability).
- **Mutual liability**: five households a unit, ten a group; informing is a legal duty — "failure to inform is bisection at the waist; informing earns the same reward as an enemy's head."
- **Religion**: the dynasty rules by Water virtue and the color black; mountain and river cults are state-regulated; necromancers grew rich on the First Emperor's quest for immortality — then the flight of two of them triggered the burying of scholars. **The fangshi is Qin's highest-return, worst-ending career**.
- **Your place**: healers and diviners sit on the legal list ("books of medicine, divination, and planting" were spared); but **debating institutions is capital**. Your political knowledge has one use in Qin — **to shut up and stay alive**.

## 12. Economy & Trade

- **The banliang coin**: one realm, one coin (round with a square hole) — the first unification of Chinese money. Memorize the standard weight and form; counterfeits and clipped coins are obvious to you.
- **Uniform weights & measures**: the chi ≈ 23.1 cm, the jin ≈ 250 g, the sheng ≈ 200 ml — from memory you are a "living standard," and granary and market clerks will want you.
- **Salt and iron are state monopolies**: all mountain-and-marsh profits belong to the crown. You may boil brine and work iron *for the government* (technicians fare far better than laborers) — never for yourself.
- **Commerce suppressed**: agriculture exalted, merchants registered and squeezed. Long-haul trade is high-risk, high-return — not your optimal path.
- **Your trading value**: medicine > agronomy > accounting & paperwork > craft. Hard currency: banliang coins, cloth, grain.

## 13. Occupations & Skills

| Skill | Value | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medicine (modern knowledge + first aid) | Extremely high (legal, wanted by all) | You already have it |
| Scribe / registrar (literacy) | High (every county office is short-staffed) | Medium (practice lishu) |
| Agronomy (iron plough, ox traction, rotation, compost) | High (the state promotes farming) | Medium |
| Artisan | Medium–high ("carve the maker's name" accountability) | Medium |
| Trade | Medium | Medium (registration and checkpoint costs) |
| Necromancer (fangshi) | Explosive returns | **Extremely dangerous** (the buried-scholars warning) |
| Divination | Medium (legal but crowded) | Low |

**Optimal strategy**: be "the literate healer." Medicine makes you irreplaceable; literacy opens the county office — two legs to stand on, steady advancement in the ordered years. If the end-times find you, the official identity becomes a liability; drop it and move.

## 14. Technology Level

- **Writing**: small-seal for office, Qin clerical for daily use; slip-and-document administration runs the realm.
- **Engineering**: the highway net "reaching Yan and Qi in the east, Wu and Chu in the south"; the Straight Road running 700-plus km to Jiuyuan; the Lingqu canal linking the Xiang and the Pearl systems.
- **Standardization**: one script, one coin, one set of weights and measures, one axle gauge; arms production under "carve the maker's name" accountability.
- **Military**: the Great Wall joining Qin, Zhao, and Yan's old ramparts; standardized crossbows, halberds, and pi blades.
- **Agriculture**: iron ploughs and ox traction universal; the Zheng Guo canal irrigates Guanzhong.
- **Calendar**: the Zhuanxu calendar, with the tenth month opening the year.
- **Medical administration**: the Grand Physician's office, leper quarantine, forensic examination forms — institutionalized medicine and inspection.

## 15. Knowledge Advantage

The following is what you "know that Qin people don't" — knowledge you can monetize from nothing:

- **Sanitation & epidemiology**: aligned with the leper-quarantine system — the least resistance you will ever meet. Camp hygiene, boiled water, wound care benefit officials and convicts alike.
- **Agronomy**: rotation, composting, seed selection, ridge-farming — under a regime that promotes agriculture, yield is a bureaucrat's achievement, and achievements mean rank.
- **Accounting & metrology**: your arithmetic and memorized standards are instant competence for granaries, treasuries, and markets.
- **First aid**: work camps never have enough people who can treat wounds, fractures, and heat casualties.
- **Geography**: memorize the highways, the Straight Road, the Wall, and the Lingqu — you will know the empire's arteries better than most officials.
- **Never flaunt**: debating enfeoffment versus commanderies ("citing the past to fault the present" — clan extermination), predicting the dynasty's fall (uncanny speech — death), military or metallurgical improvements (aiding enemies — capital). Your political knowledge has exactly one use in Qin — **silence and self-preservation**.
