> This handbook follows one core premise: **the traveler arrives empty-handed — only
> their own body and the knowledge in their head.** This page tells you how to survive
> on that alone.
>
> The Zhou is an exceptionally favorable era: the Duke of Zhou's reforms **abolished
> Shang-style human sacrifice**, and "revere virtue, protect the people" became the
> ruling creed — you are no longer a candidate for the altar. But the Zhou spans
> roughly 790 years, and **Western Zhou, Spring-Autumn, and Warring States are three
> almost different worlds**. After landing, identifying which segment you are in comes
> before everything else.

## 1. Era Snapshot

| Aspect | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| Time | c. 1046 – 256 BC (the Warring States chaos itself runs to the Qin unification, 221 BC) |
| Segments | Western Zhou (1046–771) · Spring-Autumn (770–476) · Warring States (475–221) |
| Region | Yellow River and Yangtze basins; capital Haojing (Western Zhou) → Luoyi (Eastern Zhou, modern Luoyang) |
| Polity | Enfeoffment (fengjian) + lineage law; the king sinks from overlord to figurehead; Warring States trend toward centralized bureaucracy |
| Economy | Well-field system dissolving into private land; iron and ox-ploughs from the late period; cast coinage circulates in the Warring States |
| Writing | Bronze inscriptions, great-seal script, six rival regional scripts; bamboo books spread, literacy descends to the shi class |
| Metal | Bronze ritual music at its peak; cast-iron smelting (late Spring-Autumn) — the world's earliest |

King Wu crushed the Shang at Muye in 1046 BC; the Duke of Zhou codified rites and music, built Luoyi, and enfeoffed royal kin to screen the realm. The 841 BC revolt of the guoren and the ensuing Gonghe regency mark **the start of exact chronology in China**. In 771 BC King You fell to the Quanrong; King Ping's eastward move reduced the king to a puppet: the five hegemons of the Spring-Autumn "honored the king and repelled the barbarians," the seven powers of the Warring States reformed and fought, until Qin extinguished the Zhou in 256 BC (the wars ran on to Qin's unification in 221 BC).

## 2. Before You Go

- **Identify your segment first**: Western Zhou is a tightly ordered world of rites; the Spring-Autumn is aristocratic chariot warfare with decorous rivalry; the Warring States is an age of household registration, total war, and reform. Survival strategies differ completely by segment (noted per section below).
- **Human sacrifice is abolished**: Zhou rites replaced Shang blood sacrifice with "revere virtue, protect the people" — you are no longer systematically at risk of the altar. But noble burial sacrifice persists (Duke Mu of Qin was buried with 177 retainers); stay alert.
- **Class mobility has begun**: the shi class rises, and Warring States lords keep thousands of retainers — "eating by brains alone" becomes a legitimate career for the first time.
- **Writing has spread**: bamboo books circulate; literacy can feed you. Seal script has a clear lineage from the characters you know — far easier to learn than oracle-bone script.
- **Iron and oxen change everything** (late Spring-Autumn onward): productivity leaps, and population, cities, and war scale up in step.
- **Real doctors exist**: the witch and the physician are separating; professional healers like Bian Que appear (late Spring-Autumn to Warring States). Your medical edge remains, but your rival is now crude empirical medicine, not the diviner.
- **You have nothing**: the premise is unchanged — you arrive with only your body and your mind.

## 3. First 24 Hours

1. **Locate and date yourself**: check three things — armies (armored chariot nobles = Spring-Autumn; mixed infantry and cavalry, crossbows spanning = Warring States), money (cowries and bronze chunks = Western Zhou; spade, knife, or round coins = Warring States), cities (small walled towns = Western Zhou; Linzi-style metropolis = Warring States). Get this wrong and every later step is wrong.
2. **Water first**: the rule is unchanged — **you must boil it**. Borrow pottery and an ember; otherwise filter through sand or boil in bamboo.
3. **Clothing**: improvise from bark and hides, then acquire local dress fast. The Zhou read status through dress — looking like a commoner is far safer than looking exotic.
4. **Attach to a settlement**: enter a "state" (walled city) and hire into a workshop or household; in the countryside, join a village. In the Warring States you might seek a retainer's post under a great lord — but as a newcomer, establish yourself first with labor and craft.
5. **Prepare your story**: people ask "what lineage, what state are you from?" In Western Zhou or Spring-Autumn, be "a commoner who lost his lineage"; in the Warring States, "descendant of a declined shi family" — and reciting a few lines of the Odes as proof beats any explanation.

## 4. First 30 Days

- **Learn the standard speech (yayan)**: the lingua franca of the shi class and above. Month-one goal: understand commands, recite your story, state your needs.
- **Master a trade**: farming (biggest demand), crafts (pottery, bone, weaving), or commerce (brisk in the Warring States). If literate, seek apprenticeship as a scribe (in high demand by then).
- **Observe power**: lord — minister — household steward; patronage at every layer. In the Warring States, also read the reformers against the conservatives — backing the wrong side kills.
- **Prepare for winter**: northern winters remain lethal; stockpile millet and firewood by autumn. Banked fires, straw quilts, staying dry: see [Ancient Winter Survival](/en/guides/winter-survival/).
- **Position as "itinerant healer"**: with witchcraft and medicine separating, healing is a legitimate trade (especially by the Warring States). You no longer fight the clergy for interpretive power — you only need to speak their language (yin-yang, the six qi).

## 5. Identity & Social Status

| Tier | Role | Your position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Son of Heaven | The Zhou king; nominal overlord (powerless after the eastward move) | Untouchable |
| Lords | Dukes, marquises, earls, viscounts, barons ruling states | Please them (Spring-Autumn); a gamble (Warring States) |
| Ministers | Fief-holding ministerial houses | Your realistic ceiling |
| Shi | Lowest nobility, schooled in letters and arms | **Your ladder upward** |
| Guoren | City commoners (artisans, traders) | Your likely start (urban) |
| Yeren | Rural commoners tilling the well-fields | Your likely start (rural) |
| Slaves | Far fewer, and far less central, than under the Shang | Simply avoid |

**Western Zhou status is frozen; Warring States status is fluid.** Under Shang Yang's Qin, military merit lets anyone climb; before the Jixia Academy in Qi, one tongue can earn a minister's seat. Your modern knowledge is hard currency in the Warring States — and precisely for that reason, in volatile states, weigh the price of being conspicuous.

## 6. Language & Communication

- **Language**: Old Chinese; yayan is the standard above the shi class, while "speech varies by region" (especially in the Warring States). Learn the local tongue first, then polish your yayan.
- **Writing**: bronze script → great seal → six regional scripts (unified only under Qin). The characters you know (traditional forms especially) share a lineage with seal script — **far easier than oracle bones**; writing uses brush and bamboo slips, and penmanship is employable.
- **The Odes are social currency**: Spring-Autumn diplomacy and feasts turned on "reciting poetry to state intent" — quoting the Book of Songs aptly instantly marks you as educated. Memorize a couple dozen set pieces before departure.
- **Numbers & counting rods**: rod numerals are standard; fluency in decimal place-value marks you as a capable clerk. Your mental arithmetic will outperform everyone.
- **Key vocabulary**: as before — water, fire, food, person, come, go, good, bad; meanings first, pronunciation later.

## 7. Food & Water

| Category | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Staple | Millet in the north, rice in the south, wheat spreading; Warring States yields jump with iron ploughs and oxen |
| Commoner fare | Bean stew and greens, millet gruel, pickles; meat is rare ("meat-eaters" = the ruling class) |
| Noble fare | The "eight delicacies," ding tripods and chiming bells |
| Cooking | Steaming, boiling, roasting, pickling — **no stir-frying** (the iron wok arrives in the Song) |
| Drink | Ritual alcohol restrained by the Announcement about Drunkenness; heavy drinking revives after the Spring-Autumn |
| Water | **Must be boiled** — dense cities pollute their sources worse than ever |

**Key survival knowledge**: Warring States crowding breeds epidemics. Identifying edible wild greens (shepherd's purse, goosefoot, bracken) remains your famine lifeline — field marks and the edibility trial procedure are in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).

## 8. Clothing & Housing

- **Clothing**: jacket-and-skirt evolving into the shenyi robe; hemp for most, silk for the few. Rites encode rank in dress — **dressing correctly is half the etiquette course**.
- **Headgear**: caps for the shi and above; kerchiefs for commoners. Do not exceed your station's cap and sash.
- **Housing**: roof tiles appear under the Western Zhou, but most homes remain rammed earth, timber, and thatch. The great metropolises (Linzi, Handan, Daliang, Ying, Xianyang) hold populations in the tens of thousands, with sprawling markets.
- **Bedding**: straw mats and reed bedding. Lice and fleas, as ever — accept them.

## 9. Health & Medicine

**The rival has changed: no longer the diviner, but crude empirical medicine. Your edge still dominates.**

- **Real medicine is budding**: Yi He's "six qi" theory of illness, Bian Que's four diagnostic methods, the Rites of Zhou's specialization into dieticians, physicians, and surgeons. They are rivals — and your vocabulary shell: **explain your disinfection and isolation in yin-yang and six-qi terms**, not "germs."
- **Hygiene is still miraculous**: boiling water, cleaning wounds, isolating the sick, burning plague dead. Urban density makes these worth more than in the Shang.
- **Battlefield first aid is Warring States hard currency**: pressure hemostasis, splinting, wound cleaning, burn care — every army lacks such people. It is your fastest ladder from commoner, and what may drag you to the front; weigh it.
- **Herbs**: the "mind-carried pharmacy" (mugwort, plantain, dandelion) is unchanged; identification marks in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).
- **Psychology**: the Shang's horror was the altar; the Zhou's is the battlefield — at Changping, records claim over 400,000 surrendered soldiers were buried alive (figures debated, scale unprecedented). Desensitize yourself to mass-casualty scenes before departure ([Mental Preparation](/en/guides/mental-preparation/)).
- **Childbirth & dental**: clean-hand midwifery with a boiled cutting tool, willow-twig tooth cleaning — the core bargaining chips stand.

## 10. Safety & Risks

| Risk | Level | Response (knowledge only) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conscription (Warring States total war) | High | Protected artisan/healer status defers it; military merit makes it a gamble you can choose |
| Collective-punishment law (Legalist codes) | High | Never hide from the registers or the mutual-liability units; extreme caution inside Qin |
| Epidemics (urban crowding) | High | Boil, isolate, burn the plague dead — your home ground |
| Wound infection | Lethal | Boiled-water rinse + clean dressing + isolation |
| Famine | Medium | Identify edible plants; store grain; learn fishing/trapping |
| Backing the wrong faction | High | Watch for half a year before attaching to any house |

**Female travelers**: the rites' "separation of men and women" tightens, but the Spring-Autumn still stands closer to older, freer customs than later Song-Ming times; medicine, divination, weaving, and trade are all open to women, and widow remarriage is unremarkable. Risks remain — attaching to a great household and practicing a trade ("healer, diviner") is the optimal path. See [Female Traveler's Guide](/en/guides/female-traveler/).

## 11. Laws & Religion

- **From rites to law**: Western Zhou governs by rites and virtue, "bright virtue, careful punishment," no written code; in 536 BC Zi Cheng casts the penal bronze — written law begins; by the Warring States, the Canon of Laws and Shang Yang's statutes bring collective liability and mutilating punishments.
- **Beliefs**: the mandate of Heaven — "the mandate is not constant; it sides with the virtuous"; ancestor cults and the altars of soil and grain proceed together. Tortoise and yarrow divination (the Changes) persist, but no longer gate every action.
- **Burial sacrifice persists, figures rise**: noble tombs still take retainers (Duke Mu of Qin; the tomb of Marquis Yi of Zeng), but figurines gradually substitute for the living. You cannot stop individual cases — keep a calm face.
- **The rites are a behavioral code**: the ding hierarchy (nine ding and eight gui for the king, seven for lords, five for ministers, three or one for shi), five grades of mourning garb — ritual overreach is a political crime. **Know the etiquette for your assumed rank, and always act half a rank below it.**
- **Your place**: healer, diviner, astrologer, physiognomist — all may practice openly; in the Warring States you might even found your own school — but in Legalist states (Qin above all), speak and act with care; do not become a target.

## 12. Economy & Trade

- **Money in three leaps**: Western Zhou still uses cowries and bronze chunks; the hollow-handled spade coin appears in the late Spring-Autumn; by the Warring States, spade money, knife money, round coins, Chu's "ant-nose" coins and gold plates (ying yuan) all circulate — **memorize each state's coinage**; it is instantly useful on arrival.
- **Commerce booms**: Zigong rides four-in-hand; Fan Li triples fortunes; Warring States markets "shed sweat like rain." Merchant caravans cross borders and are your lawful channel of movement.
- **Salt and iron are strategic**: Guan Zhong's state monopoly on "mountains and seas" opens the salt-and-iron profits; Handan and Wan become iron centers. Your geographic memory can locate salt sources and teach brine-boiling — **but iron and crossbows are war materiel; touching them privately inside Qin means death**.
- **Your trading value**: medicine > agronomy > hydraulic engineering > literacy > muscle. Water-engineering knowledge (flow division, sediment flushing, silt fertilization) is a treasure of state grade in the Warring States — the Korean engineer Zheng Guo built Qin's great canal as a spy and still blessed it for ten thousand generations; that is the market rate.
- **Hard currency**: gold (Chu's plates), bronze coins, cloth, salt. At the era's end, grain and salt are realest.

**Optimal strategy**: establish as an "itinerant healer," accumulate fame and patronage through battlefield first aid; then rise to technical counselor via agronomy and hydraulics. But Warring States lords discard their tools quickly — Bai Qi, Shang Yang, Wu Qi all died badly. **Be the engineer-counselor, never the power-broker.**

## 14. Technology Level

- **Iron**: wrought iron appears late in the Western Zhou; cast-iron smelting is mastered in the late Spring-Autumn (world's earliest); iron farm tools spread across the Warring States.
- **Ox-ploughing**: spreads through the era, joining the iron plough in an agricultural revolution.
- **Hydraulics**: the Han gorge canal (486 BC), Dujiangyan (256 BC), the Zheng Guo canal (246 BC, post-Zhou) — dams, flow division, and sediment management mastered.
- **Bronze**: shifts from ritual to coinage; the Zeng Hou Yi bells (433 BC) with their two tones per bell and lost-wax casting reach perfection.
- **Astronomy**: the 28 lunar mansions system forms (mansion names on the Zeng Hou Yi lacquer box); the Spring-Autumn Annals record 37 eclipses; Halley's comet logged in 613 BC; Gan De and Shi Shen compile star catalogs (among the world's earliest).
- **Mathematics**: counting rods, decimal place-value, fractions, the gou-gu (Pythagorean) rule, and the engineering standards of the Artificers' Record.
- **Medicine**: the four diagnostics, specialization, the six-qi theory — theory begins.
- **Military**: chariot warfare (Spring-Autumn) → mixed infantry and cavalry (Warring States); bronze crossbow triggers; long walls from Chu's square wall and Qi's rampart to the Warring States borders.
- **Writing**: brush, bamboo, silk; the Hundred Schools' books — "learning filling five carts" becomes possible.

## 15. Knowledge Advantage

The following is what you "know that Zhou people don't" — knowledge you can monetize from nothing:

- **Sanitation & epidemiology**: boiling, isolation, burning the plague dead. Warring States urban density gives epidemiological common sense its first real stage.
- **Agronomy**: ridge-fallow, rotation, composting, seed selection — with iron and oxen, enough to let a county "support itself for years," exactly what every reforming state craves.
- **Hydraulic principles**: flow division, sediment flushing, bend hydraulics, silt fertilization. You can land Dujiangyan-grade ideas ("dredge deep, keep the weir low") centuries early.
- **Battlefield first aid**: hemostasis, splinting, debridement, burn care — scarce skills in an age of total war.
- **Astronomy & calendrics**: the court astronomers now include Gan-and-Shi-class rivals, so your edge shifts from "sole possessor" to "precision crushes" — **switch strategy to collaboration as a calendrist, not miracle-mongering**.
- **Mathematics & measurement**: mental arithmetic, geometry, statistical bookkeeping — instant competence in any government office.
- **The Odes and etiquette**: memorized verses and manners are your cheapest passport on arrival.

**Knowledge you must NOT flaunt**: crossbow or smelting improvements (war materiel — Qin law kills), Legalist power-play performances (they flip on you fast), and predictions like "all under Heaven will go to Qin" (the crime of sedition).
