> This handbook follows one core premise: **the traveler arrives empty-handed — only
> their own body and the knowledge in their head.** This page tells you how to survive
> on that alone.
>
> The Zhou is an exceptionally favorable era: the Duke of Zhou's reforms **abolished
> Shang-style human sacrifice**, and "revere virtue, protect the people" became the
> ruling creed — you are no longer a candidate for the altar. But the dynasty spans
> roughly 790 years, and **Western Zhou, Spring-Autumn, and Warring States are three
> almost different worlds**. This page is the dynasty overview, centered on the
> **Western Zhou (1046–771 BC)**; the two Eastern Zhou eras have their own pages:
> [[spring-and-autumn-period|Spring and Autumn]] and [[warring-states-period|Warring States]].

## 1. Era Snapshot

| Segment | Time | Where to go |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Western Zhou | 1046 – 771 BC | **This page** (a world of ritual order) |
| Spring-Autumn | 770 – 476 BC | The [[spring-and-autumn-period|Spring and Autumn]] page (aristocracy's twilight, hegemon politics) |
| Warring States | 475 – 221 BC | The [[warring-states-period|Warring States]] page (total war and the Hundred Schools; Zhou fell 256 BC, the chaos runs to 221) |

| Aspect | Western Zhou |
| --- | --- |
| Region | Yellow and Yangtze basins; capital Haojing (near modern Xi'an), eastern seat Luoyi |
| Polity | Enfeoffment + lineage law: "enfeoff relatives and kin to screen the Zhou" |
| Economy | Well-field system; cowries and bronze chunks; crafts serve the royal and noble houses |
| Writing | Bronze inscriptions, great-seal script; a monopoly of scribes and diviners |
| Metal | Bronze ritual music flourishing; wrought iron appears at the dynasty's close |

King Wu crushed the Shang at Muye in 1046 BC; the Duke of Zhou codified rites and music, built Luoyi, and enfeoffed royal kin to screen the realm. King Li's tyranny provoked the 841 BC guoren revolt and the Gonghe regency — **exact Chinese chronology begins here**. King You fell to the Quanrong in 771 BC, ending the Western Zhou; the next year King Ping moved east, and the king sank toward figurehead status.

## 2. Before You Go

- **Identify your segment first**: Western Zhou is a tightly ordered world of rites; the Spring-Autumn is aristocratic chariot warfare with decorous rivalry; the Warring States is registration, total war, and reform. The strategies differ completely — identification keys are on the two era pages.
- **Human sacrifice is abolished**: Zhou rites replaced Shang blood sacrifice with "revere virtue, protect the people" — you are no longer systematically at risk of the altar. But noble burial sacrifice persists; stay alert.
- **Everything runs on rites**: the Western Zhou has no written law — rites are the behavioral code: dress, ding counts, bowing, forms of address, all graded. **Performing every ritual half a rank below your station is the iron rule**.
- **Status is frozen**: your birth fixes your destination. Ladders open only with the Spring-Autumn's private schools and the Warring States' merit ranks.
- **Writing is monopolized**: bronze script and great-seal belong to scribes and diviners. Literacy is a scarce privilege here.
- **You have nothing**: the premise is unchanged — you arrive with only your body and your mind.

## 3. First 24 Hours

1. **Locate and date yourself**: orderly, no lords at war = Western Zhou; covenant-and-hegemon politics = Spring-Autumn; reform and total war = Warring States. Misdate yourself and every strategy fails.
2. **Water first**: the eternal rule — **you must boil it**. Borrow pottery and an ember; otherwise filter through sand or boil in bamboo.
3. **Clothing**: jacket-and-skirt, hair bound, right lapel; acquire local hemp dress fast. The Zhou read status through dress — looking like a commoner is safest.
4. **Attach to a settlement**: enter a "yi" as "a commoner who lost his lineage." The Zhou care about lineage, but labor levies are constant and every settlement is short of hands.
5. **Do not discuss your origin**: prepare a lineage story — people ask "what lineage, what state?" Answer: "fleeing the Rong and Di, I lost my registration."

## 4. First 30 Days

- **Learn the standard speech (yayan)**: the lingua franca above the shi class. Month one: understand commands, recite your story, state your needs.
- **Master a trade**: farming (biggest demand) or crafts (pottery, bone, weaving). There is no free commerce to speak of in the Western Zhou.
- **Observe power**: lord — minister — household steward; the settlement's manager is the "steward" (zai).
- **Prepare for winter**: northern winters remain lethal; stock millet and firewood by autumn — see [Ancient Winter Survival](/en/guides/winter-survival/).
- **Position as "the healer among commoners"**: establish yourself with medicine and craft. Medicine and magic are not yet separated; your disinfection will be received as a kind of healing charm — stay modest.

## 5. Identity & Social Status

| Tier | Role | Your position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Son of Heaven | The Zhou king, true overlord | Untouchable |
| Lords | Dukes through barons ruling fiefs | Must be pleased |
| Ministers | Fief-holding houses | Your ceiling |
| Shi | Lowest nobility | Admire from afar |
| Commoners | Town and field laborers | **Your starting identity** |
| Bondsmen | Household slaves and serfs | Simply avoid |

**Western Zhou status is frozen**: a commoner almost never becomes a shi. Your goal is not ascent but becoming "the protected healer/artisan of such-and-such settlement." The ladders open in the [[spring-and-autumn-period|Spring and Autumn]] (private schools budding) and the [[warring-states-period|Warring States]] (commoners turned chancellor).

## 6. Language & Communication

- **Language**: Old Chinese; yayan above the shi class, "different sounds" across regions. Learn the local speech first.
- **Writing**: bronze inscriptions (cast on vessels) and great-seal script, monopolized by scribes and diviners. Your modern characters share roots with seal script — you can self-teach, but there is nowhere to spend it: **literacy buys no meals in the Western Zhou**.
- **Rites are the lingua franca**: bowing, forms of address, seating order — not overstepping matters more than speaking well.
- **Numbers**: counting rods standard; decimal arithmetic mature.
- **Key vocabulary**: water, fire, food, person, come, go, good, bad — the standing list.

## 7. Food & Water

| Category | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Staple | Millet in the north, rice in the south, wheat spreading |
| Commoner fare | Bean stew and greens, millet gruel; meat is rare |
| Cooking | Steaming, boiling, roasting, pickling — **no stir-frying** (the iron wok arrives in the Song) |
| Drink | For rites and noble feasts; commoners rarely touch it |
| Water | **Must be boiled** |

**Key survival knowledge**: identifying edible wild greens (shepherd's purse, goosefoot, bracken) remains your famine lifeline — field marks and the edibility trial procedure are in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).

## 8. Clothing & Housing

- **Clothing**: jacket-and-skirt; hemp for most, silk for the few. Rites encode rank in dress — **dressing correctly is half the etiquette course**.
- **Housing**: rammed-earth foundations, timber frames, thatch; tiled roofs for nobles appear under the Western Zhou. Live inside a walled settlement — not alone in the wild (Rong, Di, and beasts).
- **Bedding**: mats on the floor. Lice and fleas as ever — accept them.

## 9. Health & Medicine

**Medicine and magic are one — your knowledge will circulate as "healing charms."**

- **Hygiene is still miraculous**: boiling water, cleaning wounds, isolating the sick. Deliver results quietly; do not explain principles.
- **Herbs, midwifery, dental**: the standing plan shared with the Xia and Shang pages; identification marks in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).
- **Psychology**: with human sacrifice gone, gore is limited under the Western Zhou order (animal sacrifice, executions). The pressure comes from rigid rites and labor levies — demands lower than the Shang or the Warring States. Still complete [Mental Preparation](/en/guides/mental-preparation/) first.
- **Professional medicine arrives only in the [[spring-and-autumn-period|Spring and Autumn]]** (the physicians Huan and He), and its theory in the [[warring-states-period|Warring States]] (Bian Que's four diagnostics).

## 10. Safety & Risks

| Risk | Level | Response (knowledge only) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rong/Di raids (worst on the northwest frontier) | High | Live inside walls; shelter with the townsfolk |
| Labor levies (constant) | Medium–high | Healer/artisan status lightens them |
| Wound infection | Lethal | Boiled-water rinse + clean dressing + isolation |
| Famine | Medium | Wild-plant identification; grain storage |
| Ritual overreach as crime | High | Always perform the rites half a rank below |
| End-of-dynasty turmoil (around 841 / 771 BC) | High | If you land near those years, shelter eastward in Qi or Lu |

**Female travelers**: rites are founded but not yet harsh; women manage households and take part in sacrifice. The dangers come mainly from war and levies. Anchor to a settlement's great family; live by medicine and weaving. See [Female Traveler's Guide](/en/guides/female-traveler/).

## 11. Laws & Religion

- **Government by rites**: no written code — the rites are the law. "Bright virtue, careful punishment" is the slogan; the punishments (branding, nose-cutting, foot-cutting, castration, death) remain corporal.
- **Beliefs**: the mandate of Heaven — "the mandate is not constant; it sides with the virtuous"; ancestor cults and the altars of soil and grain. Divination persists, but no longer gates everything as under the Shang.
- **Burial sacrifice lingers**: noble tombs still take retainers, with figurines gradually substituting. Keep a calm face.
- **The ding hierarchy**: nine ding and eight gui for the king, seven for lords, five for ministers, three or one for shi — **overreach is a political crime**.
- **Your place**: healing and divination are legal; but the interpretation of Heaven belongs to the king and his scribes and diviners. Be a craftsman, never a priest.

## 12. Economy & Trade

- **No free market**: the "hundred crafts" serve the royal and noble houses; exchange runs on cowries, bronze chunks, and cloth.
- **The well-field system**: commoners till the public field together and pay tribute from their plots. All your agronomy must be monetized by "raising the settlement's yield."
- **Salt remains precious**: your salt-geography memory still pays — teaching brine-boiling buys long-term shelter.
- **Your trading value**: medicine > agronomy > craft > muscle. Hard currency: cowries, bronze, cloth, salt.

## 13. Occupations & Skills

| Skill | Value | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medicine (modern knowledge) | Extremely high | You already have it |
| Agronomy (seed selection, composting, fallowing) | High | Low |
| Pottery, bone-work, weaving | High | Medium |
| Farm labor | Medium | Low |
| Literacy (scribe's learning) | Low (nowhere to spend it) | High |

**Optimal strategy**: be "the healer among commoners" — medicine makes the settlement need you; agronomy makes the lord need you. Do not fight for rank; bank irreplacability. When the Eastern Zhou opens, these savings suddenly appreciate (see the "ladders" on the two era pages).

## 14. Technology Level

- **Bronze**: ritual music vessels (ding, gui, bell-chimes) at their peak; lost-wax casting present.
- **Agriculture**: wood, stone, and bone tools still carry the fields; fallow rotation; wrought iron appears at the dynasty's close.
- **Astronomy**: the king "observes the signs and grants the seasons"; intercalation refines.
- **Mathematics**: counting rods mature.
- **Writing**: bronze inscriptions record merit; great-seal script consolidates.
- **Transport**: no imperial highways yet — boats and feet; the "king's highways smooth as whetstones" cover trunk routes only.

## 15. Knowledge Advantage

The following is what you "know that Western Zhou people don't" — knowledge you can monetize from nothing:

- **Hygiene & disinfection**: boiling, wound care, isolation — delivered quietly under the guise of healing arts.
- **Agronomy**: seed selection, composting, fallow rotation — zero-cost yield gains every lord loves.
- **Simple engineering**: water channels, drying racks, kiln improvements.
- **Astronomy**: Western Zhou astronomy records but does not predict — your eclipse forecasts remain astonishing, but wrap them as "Heaven's warning; cultivate virtue."
- **Mathematics**: rod-arithmetic speed, instant competence for granaries and field surveys.
- **Never flaunt**: commentary on the ritual system (overreach), metallurgy (state-run), political prophecy (uncanny speech).

## 16. Knowledge & Skills to Memorize Before Departure (Checklist)

This is not a packing list — you can bring nothing. These are the **knowledge and skills you must load into your mind** before you go:

**Medical knowledge**
- [ ] Memorize: all drinking water must be boiled; all wounds must be cleaned with boiled water
- [ ] Know the **field identification marks** of 10+ medicinal plants — check against [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/)
- [ ] Midwifery (clean hands + boiled tool), fracture fixation, pressure hemostasis

**Geography & orientation**
- [ ] Memorize Haojing, Luoyi, and the major fiefs (Qi and Lu east, Jin north, Chu south, Qin west)
- [ ] Memorize salt sources (the Xiezhou salt lake and others)
- [ ] Learn star-based navigation (Polaris, the Big Dipper)

**Agriculture & craft**
- [ ] The three principles: seed selection, composting, fallow rotation
- [ ] Cordage, basket-weaving, friction-fire — procedure in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/)

**Mind & special topics**
- [ ] Basic desensitization (animal sacrifice, executions)
- [ ] Winter triad: banked fire, dry grass, stay dry ([Ancient Winter Survival](/en/guides/winter-survival/))
- [ ] Female travelers additionally read [Female Traveler's Guide](/en/guides/female-traveler/)

**Language & society**
- [ ] Memorize the sixty-entry ganzhi table and a few Book of Songs set pieces (Eastern Zhou social currency)
- [ ] Know the ding hierarchy and mourning grades: always perform half a rank below
- [ ] Prepare the persona: "a commoner who lost his registration fleeing the Rong"
- [ ] If landing in the Eastern Zhou: add the specialized checklists of the [[spring-and-autumn-period|Spring and Autumn]] or [[warring-states-period|Warring States]] page
