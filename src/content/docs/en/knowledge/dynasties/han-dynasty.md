> This handbook follows one core premise: **the traveler arrives empty-handed — only
> their own body and the knowledge in their head.** This page tells you how to survive
> on that alone.
>
> The Han is the **first long-lived empire**: for over four hundred years, "Han" is
> simply the word for order itself. Mutilating punishments abolished, a recommendation
> system, an imperial academy — the traveler receives an **institutional ladder upward**
> for the first time. But those four centuries are really **seven different worlds**:
> the mildness of Wen and Jing, the extraction under Emperor Wu, the madness of Wang
> Mang, and the plagues and warlords of the finale — **nowhere else in this handbook
> does the landing date matter more**.

## 1. Era Snapshot

| Phase | Time | The world |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early Han · Wen–Jing | 202 – 141 BC | Friendliest: light taxes and corvée, trade barriers opened |
| Emperor Wu | 141 – 87 BC | Extraction: salt-iron monopoly, property denunciation, forty years of war |
| Zhao–Xuan to Ai–Ping | 87 – 8 BC | Revival then decay: Zhao-Xuan restoration → the Wang consort clan grows |
| Xin (Wang Mang) | AD 9 – 23 | **Disaster**: reform and currency both collapse; Red Eyebrows and Lülin revolt |
| Eastern Han height | 25 – 105 | Order rebuilt: Guangwu to Emperor He; Buddhism arrives, paper improves |
| Eastern Han decline | 106 – 183 | Fatigue: Qiang wars, Partisan Prohibitions, manorial magnates |
| The end | 184 – 220 | **Hell**: Yellow Turbans, Dong Zhuo, warlords, and plague |

| Aspect | Summary |
| --- | --- |
| Region | Western Han capital Chang'an, Eastern Han Luoyang; Hexi Corridor absorbed, a Protectorate over the Western Regions from 60 BC |
| Polity | Emperor + Three Ministers and Nine Courts (the Imperial Secretariat rises in the east); commanderies and counties with hollowed-out kingdoms |
| Economy | Land tax one-thirtieth, plus poll taxes (suanfu, koufu) and corvée; salt and iron state-run after Emperor Wu |
| Writing | Clerical script (Han lishu) at its height — the direct ancestor of the characters you know; late Han drifts toward regular and running hands |
| Belief | Confucianism as state orthodoxy; prognostication texts rampant (especially in the east); Buddhism enters, Daoism stirs |

Liu Bang took the throne in 202 BC and ruled from Chang'an; the Han "inherited Qin institutions" but loosened the net: the Wen-Jing reigns rested the people, the Seven Kingdoms were crushed in 154 BC. Emperor Wu struck the Xiongnu, opened the Western Regions, and made Confucianism sole orthodoxy — while running the state to its extraction limit. After the Zhao-Xuan restoration the consort Wang clan grew until Wang Mang usurped in AD 9; his reforms and currency both collapsed. Liu Bang's descendant Liu Xiu rebuilt the Han at Luoyang in 25; after the Ming and Zhang reigns, Qiang wars and the Partisan Prohibitions wore it down. The Yellow Turbans rose in 184, plague and warlords followed, and in 220 Cao Pi ended the four-hundred-year house (the Three Kingdoms after that will get their own page).

## 2. Before You Go

- **Four centuries = seven worlds**: on the same ground, the survival strategy for Wen-Jing and for the final years are opposites. Your first move is always "date your landing" (see §3) — get it wrong and everything after is wrong.
- **The state remains, but gentler**: registered households ("ming-shu") and checkpoint permits ("zhuan") persist — yet Emperor Wen abolished mutilating punishments (167 BC), Emperor Xuan let relatives shield one another from prosecution, and amnesties for displaced people to register themselves were routine. **Voluntarily registering is the correct path**; being caught unregistered earns convict status.
- **For the first time, literacy equals a career**: recommendation for office (xiaolian), Academy students, clerical service in counties — your modern character base (via Han clerical script) can be converted directly into official employment. Nothing like it existed before.
- **Medicine is now a system — you are no longer the only one who knows**: the *Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon* and the *Divine Farmer's Materia Medica* exist, and at the end Zhang Zhongjing and Hua Tuo. Your territory is aseptic surgery, epidemic control, and obstetric hygiene — fields they entirely lack.
- **Merchants gamble on policy**: early Han opened the passes and relaxed the mountains-and-marshes bans; Emperor Wu turned around with the property-denunciation system ("middle-rank merchant families mostly broke") and the salt-iron monopoly. In the east, free farmers also face enclosure by the magnates.
- **The finale (184–220) is top-tier difficulty for this whole handbook**: Yellow Turbans, Dong Zhuo, warlord wars stacked on repeated great plagues (Zhang Zhongjing's clan lost two-thirds in ten years). If you land here, read §10 first.
- **You have nothing**: the premise is unchanged — you arrive with only your body and your mind.

## 3. First 24 Hours

1. **Date your landing — more important here than in any other era**:
   - **Coins**: crisp, uniform wuzhu coins = normal Han; a jumble of "huoquan" and "huobu" denominations = Wang Mang's Xin; clipped, shrunken coins or trade in grain and cloth = anarchy (late Xin or late Han).
   - **Rumors**: capital in the west (Chang'an) or east (Luoyang)? Xiongnu marriage-alliance or a submissive chanyu? Any "The Azure Heaven is dead" graffiti (= around 184)? Buddhist monks and temples in town (gradually after AD 68)?
   - **Ask policy buzzwords, not reign names**: "light land tax" = Wen-Jing; "property levies" = Emperor Wu; "Qiang wars" or "the partisans" = late Eastern Han. Cross-check, then commit.
2. **Water first**: the rule is eternal — **you must boil it**.
3. **Clothing**: a commoner's short jacket and loincloth will do. Western Han wraps diagonally, Eastern Han straight — copy your neighbors; the Han care about proper dress, so do not stand out.
4. **Register — "self-declare your household"**: find the township clerk (sefu) and declare yourself "a subject who lost his registration in the troubles." Amnesties inviting displaced people to register were routine; **voluntary declaration is the way to live**, being caught unregistered is convict labor.
5. **Do not discuss your origin**: displaced people never stop flowing across four hundred years (floods, corvée, enclosure, war) — "a refugee" is the one identity that needs no explanation in the Han.

## 4. First 30 Days

- **Learn the language and script**: the common tongue ("tongyu") plus local dialects; drill Han clerical script starting from the *Jijiupian* (the Han literacy primer). Clerical Han is the direct ancestor of your characters — the lowest barrier since the Qin.
- **Map township power**: the Three Elders (moral instruction), the sefu (taxes and disputes), the youjiao (policing), a pavilion chief every ten li — these are your direct superiors.
- **Master a trade**: medicine (add local materia medica to what you know), farming (you can improve on the dai-tian furrow system and the seed drill), crafts (iron, paper).
- **Prepare for winter**: unchanged — stock millet and firewood by autumn; see [Ancient Winter Survival](/en/guides/winter-survival/).
- **Position as "the literate surgeon"**: literacy buys a future, surgery saves lives — the Han version of walking on two legs.

## 5. Identity & Social Status

| Tier | Role | Your position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emperor | The Liu house | Untouchable |
| Consort kin / eunuchs | Western Han in-laws, Eastern Han eunuchs alternate in power | Keep a polite distance |
| Officials | Via recommendation, Academy, military merit, or purchase | Your target circle |
| Magnate landlords | Great estates with private muscle (eastern Han above all) | Attach, never oppose |
| Registered commoners | Freehold farmers | **Your starting identity** |
| Estate dependents / retainers | Tied to manors, de facto serfs | Where bankrupt farmers end up |
| Slaves | Numerous state and private bondservants | Simply avoid |

**Ladders upward (open to commoners for the first time since high antiquity)**: recommendation as Filial and Incorrupt (under Eastern Han rules, one nominee per ~200,000 people), Academy students passing examination into clerkships, promotion by accumulated merit from county clerk, military merit on the frontier, or purchasing office. Literacy plus one classic — or medicine — moves you from farmer into the official class: the Han's greatest gift to the traveler.

## 6. Language & Communication

- **Language**: the common tongue prevails, dialects still diverge widely (Yang Xiong's *Fangyan* records them). Local speech first, standard pronunciation second.
- **Writing**: Han clerical script at its height; slips and tablets are the medium, silk is dear, hemp paper spreads (faster after Cai Lun's improvements in AD 105). Han lishu is the direct ancestor of the modern character — your reading advantage peaks in the Han.
- **The literacy ecosystem**: the *Jijiupian* is the primer, Xu Shen's *Shuowen Jiezi* (finished AD 100) the dictionary, and from 175 the Xiping Stone Classics stand outside the Academy — public, standard texts carved for all to copy.
- **Naming detail**: the Han call the emperor "xianguan" ("the county official") or Son of Heaven; after Wang Mang's ban on two-character personal names, single names became the norm (almost everyone in the east has one) — **give yourself a single-character name**.
- **Numbers**: counting rods plus the *Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art* already solve equations and use negative numbers — yet your decimal written arithmetic is still a different league of speed.
- **Key vocabulary**: water, fire, food, person, come, go, good, bad — the standing list.

## 7. Food & Water

| Category | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Staple | Millet, wheat, rice; wheaten food rising ("tangbing," a strip soup — noodles' ancestor) |
| Side | Bean stew, pickles, green soup; slightly more meat on eastern estates, still rare for commoners |
| Cooking | Steaming, boiling, roasting, pickling — **still no stir-frying** (the iron wok arrives in the Song) |
| New ingredients | Grapes and alfalfa arrive over the Western Regions (after Emperor Wu) — seeing them on the market means you are at least in the 2nd century BC or later |
| Salt | State-run but sold openly at stable prices; private boiling of brine remains a grave crime |
| Water | **Must be boiled** |

**Key survival knowledge**: tofu is *said* to hail from Prince Liu An of Huainan (unproven — don't count on it). Wild-greens identification (shepherd's purse, goosefoot, bracken) remains your famine lifeline — field marks in [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/).

## 8. Clothing & Housing

- **Clothing**: a commoner's short jacket and loincloth; elite shenyi robes (diagonal wrap in the west, straight in the east) give way to the ao gown. Silk is more common but still precious — you wear hemp. Crotched trousers have arrived; no more Shang-era leggings.
- **Housing**: rammed-earth walls, timber frame, and tiled roofs are now ordinary; the typical cottage is "one hall, two inner rooms." **The fortified manor (wubao) of the eastern Han is the best shelter in an age of chaos** — walls, grain, and retainers.
- **Cities**: Chang'an's nine markets crown the west; Luoyang leads the east, ranked with Handan, Linzi, Wan, and Chengdu as the "Five Capitals." Markets have their tower, their officer, and their merchant registers.
- **Bedding**: floor seating, rising couches, low tables for meals; the folding camp stool only enters the palace at the very end. Lice and fleas, as ever — accept them.

## 9. Health & Medicine

**The shift: from "the only one who knows medicine" to "the one who knows the half they don't."**

- **The system exists**: the *Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon* supplies theory, the *Divine Farmer's Materia Medica* lists 365 drugs, Chunyu Yi ("Canggong") already kept case records; at the end come Zhang Zhongjing's *Treatise on Cold Damage* and Hua Tuo's surgery and "five-animal" exercises. Internal medicine and pharmacology carry two thousand years of momentum — **do not try to outplay them on their ground**.
- **Their blank spots are your stronghold**: aseptic wound care, fracture setting, epidemic control (isolation, water protection, handling the dead), obstetric hygiene (hand-washing, boiling instruments). Han medicine has none of it — one demonstration and it is yours alone.
- **Physicians are respectable**: the Grand Physician heads the medical administration; attendants and common practitioners all make a living with dignity. "Literate + surgeon" is the ceiling of the Han job market.
- **Red lines**: witchcraft (wugu) is a capital crime (the 91 BC witchcraft purge killed tens of thousands), "uncanny speech" is still criminal — beyond medicine, perform no "miracles."
- **The great plagues of the end (repeated 171–185, then 217)**: Zhang Zhongjing's clan of 200-plus lost two-thirds in a decade; Cao Zhi wrote "every house mourns a corpse." If you land in the finale, your isolation and quarantine knowledge is world-saving — and your own ticket to survive it.
- **Mentally**: public executions and unburied corpses are ordinary sights; complete desensitization before departure ([Mental Preparation](/en/guides/mental-preparation/)).

## 10. Safety & Risks

| Risk | Level | Response (knowledge only) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing in Wang Mang's Xin (9–23) | **Deadly** | Currency wrecked, war coming — leave both capitals fast, hoard grain early, attach to a fortified local clan estate |
| Landing in the end times (184–220) | **Deadly** | Epidemic discipline first; keep clear of the capitals and trunk roads; shelter in a wubao, or from 196 join the state farms (a steady ration) |
| Corvée and garrison duty | Medium–high | Commutation fee (gengfu) can buy it off; medical or craft status defers it |
| Frontier wars (Xiongnu / Qiang / Wuhuan) | High | If you land in Liang, Bing, or You, assess first: marriage-alliance years are calm, war years mean moving inland |
| Magnate enclosure (eastern Han) | High | Freeholder bankruptcy is the norm — attach to an estate early rather than farm alone |
| Witchcraft / uncanny-speech accusation | High | Practice medicine only; no "magic" |
| Epidemic disease | High (deadly at the end) | Boiling, isolation, quarantine — your home ground |
| Famine | Medium | Wild greens, grain stores |

**Female travelers note**: the Han is a high-water mark for women's standing — the Zhangjiashan statutes confirm a widow could head and inherit a household, Zhuo Wenjun drew crowds selling wine at her own counter, remarriage raised no eyebrows, and dowager regencies were routine. Earn by medicine, weaving, or divination and register under a great clan's wing — details in [Female Traveler's Guide](/en/guides/female-traveler/).

## 11. Laws & Religion

- **A looser net**: Xiao He's Nine-Chapter Statutes with ordinances, statutes, and precedents; since Emperor Wen abolished mutilating punishments (167 BC, after Tiying's petition) the system runs on penal labor and flogging (castration survives — Sima Qian received it); executions wait for autumn and winter; Emperor Xuan ruled that close kin may conceal each other's crimes. A full order of magnitude more humane than Qin.
- **"Spring-and-Autumn adjudication"**: judging by intent — one grumbling remark can cost a head. Stay careful discussing politics.
- **Red lines to memorize**: counterfeiting coin (the wuzhu is the state's foundation), witchcraft, uncanny speech, forging prognostication texts, private salt-boiling.
- **Religion**: after Confucianism became orthodoxy it turned statal (the feng-sacrifice, the Bright Hall, the Academy); in the east, chenwei prognostication became "inner learning" and **forging it is a capital crime**; Buddhism, established at the White Horse Temple in 68, spreads slowly and is filed as one more Huang-Lao art; the Way of Great Peace and the Five-Pecks-of-Rice movement swell in the countryside — the 184 Yellow Turbans *were* the former, so **religious societies at the end are minefields**.
- **Your place**: medicine and divination are legal and respectable; stay away from omens and prognostication — a trade where being right earns nothing and being wrong costs your head.

## 12. Economy & Trade

- **The wuzhu coin** (first minted 118 BC): about 3.2 g, inscribed "wu zhu" — the standard currency for the next seven hundred years, crisp and hard to counterfeit.
- **Wang Mang's currency disaster**: five materials, six names, twenty-eight denominations ended in collapse and a return to grain-and-cloth exchange — also your hard clue when dating a landing.
- **"Grain and cloth as money" in the east**: cloth regains monetary function — your hard currencies are wuzhu coins, cloth, and millet.
- **Salt-iron monopoly and price stabilization** (after Emperor Wu): the mountains' and marshes' profits belong to the state. You may hire into the state works (technicians are treated far better than laborers) but not go independent.
- **The lesson of the denunciations**: under Emperor Wu, "merchant families of middling rank and above mostly went broke" — check which way the policy wind blows before taking a merchant register.
- **Your exchange value**: medicine > agronomy > paperwork and accounting > craft. Literacy is itself a commodity here — writing letters, legal records, and primers for others all pay.

## 13. Occupations & Skills

| Skill | Value | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medicine (surgery / epidemics) | Supreme (their blank field) | You have it (add local materia medica) |
| Classics + paperwork (recommendation track) | Supreme (an institutional channel) | Medium (one classic + lishu + the *Jijiupian*) |
| Agronomy (dai-tian, seed drill, qu-tian) | High (the state urges farming) | Low |
| Craft (iron; paper after 105) | Medium–high | Medium |
| Trade | Medium (policy-cycle risk) | Medium |
| Frontier military merit | Medium | High (a wager on your life) |
| Prognosticator / occultist | Explosive returns | **Extremely dangerous** (capital crime) |

**Optimal strategy**: be "the literate surgeon." In ordered times, live by medicine and climb the long recommendation ladder through letters; the moment you date a collapse (Xin, or after 184), pivot instantly — drop the identity baggage, attach to a fortified manor as its epidemic physician, and outlive the age into the next unified dynasty.

## 14. Technology Level

- **Paper**: hemp paper already exists in the western Han (Fangmatan, Baqiao); Cai Lun improves it in 105 and it spreads — but slips and tablets remain the norm; paper's victory waits for the Wei-Jin.
- **Iron**: puddled steel and "hundred-refined" blades mature; Du Shi's water-powered bellows (AD 31) — iron plows and iron weapons are universal.
- **Agriculture**: the dai-tian furrow rotation (Zhao Guo), the three-tube seed drill, intensive pit-farming (the *Fanshengzhi Manual*), ox-plowing everywhere; the Bai Canal joins the Zhengguo Canal (95 BC).
- **Mathematics**: the *Nine Chapters* solve equations with negative numbers; the *Zhoubi* discusses the gnomon and right triangles.
- **Astronomy & calendar**: the Taichu calendar (104 BC) is the first fully recorded calendar; Zhang Heng builds the armillary sphere and, in 132, the seismoscope.
- **Medicine**: the *Inner Canon* and *Materia Medica* settle into shape; at the end the *Treatise on Cold Damage* founds treatment by pattern.
- **Engineering**: beacon-line frontier systems (the Juyan slips are the archive), the Western Regions Protectorate, Wang Jing's Yellow River works (AD 70), qanat-style blind channels.
- **Transport**: the Silk Road runs end to end (Chang'an — Hexi — the Western Regions — Daqin), a pavilion every ten li, relay stations like Xuanquzhi; Gan Ying reaches the Persian Gulf for Rome in 97, and Roman envoys reach the Han court in 166.
- **Textiles**: the pattern loom exists (Western-Han loom models excavated at Laoguanshan, Chengdu); Mawangdui's plain gauze robe weighs just 49 grams.

## 15. Knowledge Advantage

What you "know but the Han do not," convertible from nothing (no objects required):

- **Epidemic control**: world-saving at the end; in normal years the barracks, the worksite, and the birthing room never have enough of it.
- **Aseptic surgery**: they have drugs and theory but no concept of germs — debridement, suturing, splinting, isolation are yours alone.
- **Agronomy**: add rotation, green manure, and seed selection on top of dai-tian — under a regime that "urges farming," yield is political achievement.
- **Arithmetic**: written decimals plus speed calculation outclass counting rods by an order of magnitude — instant value in any granary or market office.
- **Calendar and skies**: your remembered tropical year and eclipse accuracy beat the Taichu and Sifen calendars — but be "one who knows the calendar," never "one who reads the omens."
- **Geography**: the Silk Road's course, the Xiongnu's migrations, the thirty-six kingdoms of the Western Regions, the Five Capitals and frontiers — the map in your head beats most officials'.
- **The principle of paper**: bark, hemp ends, rags, fishing nets — ret, pound, scoop, dry — cheap coarse paper at any point in the era (but the slip-and-tablet ecosystem is entrenched; treat it as a side trade, not a career).
- **Never flaunt**: prognostication and omens (capital crime), taking sides in the Partisan Prohibitions (the eastern-Han official meat grinder), improving weapons (private-army suspicion), predicting dynastic change (uncanny speech).

## 16. Knowledge & Skills to Memorize Before Departure (Checklist)

This is not a packing list — you can bring nothing. This is **what must be in your head before departure**:

**Medical knowledge**
- [ ] Memorize: all drinking water must be boiled; all wounds cleaned with boiled water
- [ ] Memorize the field-identification marks of 10+ medicinal plants — review against [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/)
- [ ] Midwifery (hand-washing + boiled tools), fracture splinting, pressure hemostasis, burn care
- [ ] Extra drill: the epidemic trio (isolation, quarantine, water protection) and handling the dead — the last decades are plague years

**Geography & orientation**
- [ ] Memorize Chang'an vs. Luoyang (western / eastern capitals) and the Five Capitals (Luoyang, Handan, Linzi, Wan, Chengdu)
- [ ] Memorize the Hexi Corridor — Western Regions Protectorate line and the Silk Road trunk
- [ ] Memorize the frontiers (Xiongnu north, Qiang west, Wuhuan northeast) and the post-184 refuge directions (Jiangnan, Jing-Yi, Liaodong)

**Agriculture & crafts**
- [ ] The four principles: dai-tian furrows, qu-tian pits, rotation, green manure; how the seed drill works
- [ ] Cordage, basketry, fire-making by hand — see [Survival Skills Field Manual](/en/guides/survival-skills/)

**Mindset & special topics**
- [ ] Desensitize to public executions, plague scenes, and wartime corpses ([Mental Preparation](/en/guides/mental-preparation/))
- [ ] Winter's three tasks: bank the fire, dry grass, keep dry ([Ancient Winter Survival](/en/guides/winter-survival/))
- [ ] Female travelers also read [Female Traveler's Guide](/en/guides/female-traveler/)

**Language & society**
- [ ] **Practice Han clerical script with a brush** and skim the *Jijiupian* — literacy is your sharpest weapon
- [ ] Memorize the seven phases and their strategies (Wen-Jing: register; Emperor Wu: defer service; Xin: flee fast; the end: epidemic discipline)
- [ ] Memorize the recommendation rules (annual xiaolian quotas), the corvée commutation fee, the self-registration procedure, and your "refugee" story
- [ ] Memorize the wuzhu standard (~3.2 g, "wu zhu" legend) and metrology (chi 23.1 cm, jin 250 g, sheng 200 ml)
